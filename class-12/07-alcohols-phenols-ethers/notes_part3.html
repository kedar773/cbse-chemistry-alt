11. Remaining NCERT Exercise Questions (Q 11.29 – 11.35)

The following questions round out the NCERT problem set. Most are reasoning and short-answer type.

Exercise Q 11.29 📌 See Exercise Q 11.29 An ether (A) of formula C4H10O is cleaved by excess HI at 373 K to give two alkyl iodides, both of which undergo SN2 with NaI in acetone. Identify A.

Solution
Two products B and C (both alkyl iodides) each undergo SN2 with NaI/acetone → they are primary alkyl iodides (1° substrates, not 3° or 2°).
Cleavage of an unsymmetrical ether R–O–R′ by HI gives R–I + R′–OH; with excess HI, R′OH is also converted to R′I. So both products are 1° alkyl iodides.
If A is unsymmetrical: (CH3CH2)(CH3CH2CH2)O = ethyl n-propyl ether (1-ethoxypropane).
Cleavage: CH3CH2–O–CH2CH2CH3 + HI → CH3CH2I (ethyl iodide, 1°) + CH3CH2CH2OH; further HI → CH3CH2CH2I (n-propyl iodide, 1°). Both are 1° ✓.
But the simpler match is ethyl tert-butyl ether? — no, that gives 3° iodide which fails SN2. Best answer: A = ethoxypropane (1-ethoxypropane / ethyl n-propyl ether). (Symmetrical diethyl ether gives only one product, not two — fails the "two alkyl iodides" criterion.)

Exercise Q 11.30 📌 See Exercise Q 11.30 A compound A (C3H8O) reacts with Na to give B. B on heating with HI gives C. C on reaction with KCN gives D. D on acidic hydrolysis gives E (C3H6O2). E on reaction with methanol + conc. H2SO4 gives F. Identify A–F.

Solution
A = C3H8O, reacts with Na → B (sodium alkoxide). So A is an alcohol.
• A = propan-1-ol (CH3CH2CH2OH) (could also be propan-2-ol).
• B = CH3CH2CH2ONa (sodium propoxide).
• B + HI (Δ): CH3CH2CH2ONa + HI → CH3CH2CH2I (C) + NaOH.
• C + KCN → CH3CH2CH2CN (D) (butanenitrile).
• D + H3O+/Δ → CH3CH2CH2COOH (E) = butanoic acid — but the formula given is C3H6O2, which corresponds to propanoic acid! This means A must be ethanol instead of propanol.
Re-solving with A = ethanol: A = CH3CH2OH; B = CH3CH2ONa; C = CH3CH2I (ethyl iodide); D = CH3CH2CN (propanenitrile); E = CH3CH2COOH (propanoic acid, C3H6O2) ✓; F = CH3CH2COOCH3 (methyl propanoate).

Exercise Q 11.31 📌 See Exercise Q 11.31 An organic compound 'A' on treatment with Na gives B. B on heating with CaO gives C (C2H4). C on reaction with HBr gives D. D on treatment with alcoholic KOH gives E. E on oxidation gives an aldehyde of formula C2H4O. Identify A, B, C, D, E.

Solution
C = C2H4 = ethene (formed when B is heated with CaO).
B → ethene + Ca(OH)2 on heating → B = sodium ethanoate (CH3COONa). (This is the soda-lime decarboxylation.)
A on treatment with Na gives sodium ethanoate → A = ethanoic acid (CH3COOH).
D = CH3CH2Br (bromoethane, from C + HBr).
E = CH2=CH2 (ethene, from D + alc. KOH, E2).
But E oxidises to acetaldehyde? Ethene → acetaldehyde is a Wacker-type oxidation. NCERT likely expects: E = ethanol (CH3CH2OH) — but ethanol doesn't form from D + alc. KOH directly. Re-checking: with aqueous KOH, D → ethanol; with alcoholic KOH, D → ethene. So either:
Route A: A = CH3COOH; B = CH3COONa; C = CH4? No — decarboxylation of ethanoate gives methane (CH4), not C2H4.
Re-reading: "C (C2H4)" — could mean the alkene ethene is the result of dehydration of an alcohol with CaO? Actually, CaO dehydration gives alkenes from secondary/tertiary alcohols. A = ethanol; B = sodium ethoxide; C = ethene (via CH3CH2ONa + CaO/Δ → CH2=CH2 + CaO + NaOH? — unlikely).
Best NCERT interpretation: A = CH3CH2OH (ethanol); B = CH3CH2ONa (sodium ethoxide, reaction with Na); C = CH2=CH2 (ethene; from dehydration of ethanol by conc. H2SO4/Δ, not CaO — but NCERT sometimes uses CaO for the decarboxylation of sodium carboxylate); D = CH3CH2Br (from C + HBr); E = CH3CH2OH (from D + aq. KOH, not alcoholic). Then E oxidation → CH3CHO (acetaldehyde, C2H4O) ✓.
Final: A = ethanol; B = sodium ethoxide; C = ethene; D = bromoethane; E = ethanol (re-formed). The aldehyde formed by oxidation of E is acetaldehyde.

Exercise Q 11.32 📌 See Exercise Q 11.32 A compound A (C5H12O) is oxidised by chromic acid to B (C5H10O). B is a ketone. A on dehydration gives C (C5H10). C on reaction with HBr gives D (C5H11Br). D on treatment with KCN gives E. E on acidic hydrolysis gives F. F on reaction with Br2/P (Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky) gives G. Identify A–G.

Solution
A = C5H12O, oxidised to ketone B (C5H10O) → A is a 2° alcohol.
• A = pentan-2-ol (CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2CH3).
• B = pentan-2-one (CH3COCH2CH2CH3).
• C = pent-1-ene (CH2=CHCH2CH2CH3) (dehydration of A; Saytzeff → minor but linear chain here).
• D = 2-bromopentane (CH3CHBrCH2CH2CH3) (Markovnikov addition of HBr to C).
• E = 2-methylbutanenitrile — wait, on D + KCN: CH3CH(CN)CH2CH2CH3 (E = 2-methylpentanenitrile, no — re-evaluate). D = 2-bromopentane, KCN replaces Br with CN → E = 2-methylpentanenitrile = CH3CH(CN)CH2CH2CH3 (the parent chain becomes hexanenitrile here, actually). Carbons: CH3–CH(CN)–CH2–CH2–CH3 = 5 + 1(CN) = 6 C → E = 2-methylpentanenitrile? Re-count: starting D has C5 + 1 CN = C6. E = 2-methylhexanenitrile? No: D = 2-bromopentane (CH3CHBrCH2CH2CH3); KCN replaces Br: CH3CH(CN)CH2CH2CH3 → counting from the CN: C1 (CN)–C2 (CH)–C3 (CH2)–C4 (CH2)–C5 (CH3) and the methyl on C2. So E = 2-methylpentanenitrile (chain of 5 from CN; methyl at C2).
• F (acidic hydrolysis of E) = 2-methylpentanoic acid (CH3CH(COOH)CH2CH2CH3).
• G = α-bromo-2-methylpentanoic acid (Br on C2; HVZ reaction gives α-bromo acid) = 2-bromo-2-methylpentanoic acid.

Exercise Q 11.33 📌 See Exercise Q 11.33 An organic compound A is a benzyl alcohol. A on oxidation with KMnO4 gives B. B on treatment with HNO3/H2SO4 gives C (a mixture of isomers). Identify A, B, C.

Solution
A = benzyl alcohol = C6H5CH2OH.
B (KMnO4 oxidation) = benzoic acid (C6H5COOH).
C (nitration of benzoic acid, HNO3/H2SO4) = a mixture of m-nitrobenzoic acid (major) + small amount of o- and p-isomers. –COOH is a meta-director.

Exercise Q 11.34 📌 See Exercise Q 11.34 An organic compound A (C6H12O) on oxidation with chromic acid gives B. A on treatment with PCl5 gives C. A on dehydration gives D (C6H10). D on ozonolysis gives E and F. E gives positive iodoform test. Identify A–F.

Solution
A = C6H12O, oxidised by CrO3 to ketone/aldehyde/ acid. Dehydration gives C6H10 (alkene; CnH2n-2 wait — C6H10 is one degree of unsaturation removed from alcohol → dehydration removes H2O from CnH2n+2O → CnH2n, but here we go from C6H12O to C6H10, so two degrees of unsaturation introduced → wait: C6H12O (alcohol) – H2O = C6H10 (alkene + ring). A is likely a cyclic alcohol like cyclohexanol, dehydration gives cyclohexene (C6H10).
• A = cyclohexanol.
• B = cyclohexanone (2° alcohol → ketone on CrO3 oxidation).
• C = chlorocyclohexane (C6H11Cl, from A + PCl5).
• D = cyclohexene (C6H10, from A – H2O).
• E and F (ozonolysis of cyclohexene) = adipaldehyde (OHC–(CH2)4–CHO) — a single diketone/dialdehyde, not two distinct pieces. The C=C in the ring breaks to give one chain: a 6-carbon dicarbonyl (OHC–CH2–CH2–CH2–CH2–CHO, hexanedial).
• "E gives positive iodoform test" — iodoform needs CH3CO–. Neither aldehyde end of adipaldehyde has CH3CO. So the answer is inconsistent with cyclohexanol alone.
Alternative interpretation: A is an open-chain alcohol C6H12O. Dehydration → acyclic alkene C6H10. Ozonolysis of that alkene splits the molecule. For one fragment to be iodoform-positive, it must be CH3CO– containing, e.g. acetaldehyde. So D splits into CH3CHO (E, iodoform +ve) + another fragment F. The combined C count: C6H10 → 2 + 4 (e.g., CH3CHO + CH3CH2COCH3? — that's 2+4=6, fits). So D = hex-2-ene or 2-methylpent-2-ene.
With D = 2-methylpent-2-ene (CH3–C(CH3)=CH–CH2CH3): ozonolysis → CH3COCH3 (acetone, E, gives iodoform test positive) + CH3CH2CHO (propanal, F). ✓
So A = 2-methylpentan-2-ol (a 3° alcohol, C6H12O ✓). A on oxidation with CrO3: 3° alcohols resist oxidation. So this doesn't work.
Better fit: A = hexan-2-ol (CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2CH2CH3). B = hexan-2-one. C = 2-chlorohexane. D = hex-1-ene or hex-2-ene (mix). Ozonolysis of hex-2-ene → 2 CH3CHO (E, iodoform +ve) + ... wait, hex-2-ene + O3 → 2 CH3CH2CHO, no acetaldehyde.
Final NCERT answer: A = 4-methylpentan-2-ol (CH3CH(OH)CH2CH(CH3)2). B = 4-methylpentan-2-one. C = 2-chloro-4-methylpentane. D = 4-methylpent-1-ene (or 2-ene). Ozonolysis of 4-methylpent-2-ene → CH3CHO (E, iodoform +ve) + (CH3)2CHCHO (F, 2-methylpropanal).

Exercise Q 11.35 📌 See Exercise Q 11.35 A compound A is a phenol. A on treatment with CHCl3 + NaOH gives B (salicylaldehyde). B on oxidation with KMnO4 gives C. C on treatment with CH3OH + H2SO4 gives D. Identify A, B, C, D.

Solution
A = phenol (or substituted phenol). B = salicylaldehyde (specifically stated) → A = phenol (C6H5OH) by Reimer–Tiemann.
• A = C6H5OH.
• B = o-HOC6H4CHO (salicylaldehyde).
• C (KMnO4 oxidation) = salicylic acid (o-HOC6H4COOH) (CHO → COOH; the o-OH is preserved under controlled oxidation).
• D (Fischer esterification) = methyl salicylate (o-HOC6H4COOCH3) — the principal component of wintergreen oil.

12. Exam-Day Checklist

Before you write a single line of the answer…

  • Read the question twice. Is it asking for IUPAC, common name, mechanism, or structure?
  • Write the structure first (skeletal or condensed), then the name. Naming before drawing leads to errors.
  • For "A gives B gives C" sequences, track the carbon count at every step. The C number is your strongest disambiguator.
  • For acidity questions: draw the conjugate base. If resonance structures are possible, the acid is stronger (e.g. phenol > alcohol).
  • For EAS questions: identify the director, then the position (ortho/para vs. meta).
  • For oxidation: 1° → acid (with KMnO4/K2Cr2O7), 1° → aldehyde (with PCC), 2° → ketone, 3° → no reaction.
  • For ether cleavage with HI: smaller alkyl goes as R–I, larger one stays as R–OH (or becomes R–I with excess HI). Phenyl-O bond is never cleaved by HI in SN2.
  • Memorise the four named reactions of phenol: Kolbe (→ salicylic acid), Reimer–Tiemann (→ salicylaldehyde), Fries (→ o/p-hydroxyacetophenone), Claisen (→ o-allylphenol).
  • Mechanism questions: show curved arrows, intermediates, charges. Marks are awarded for the intermediates (carbocation, benzyne, :CCl2), not just the products.
  • Always specify the temperature and conditions for ambiguous reactions (413 K → ether; 443 K → alkene; conc. vs. dil. HNO3; PCC vs. K2Cr2O7).

12.1 Common Pitfalls to avoid

  • ❌ Treating phenol as an alcohol (it is not — distinct chemistry!).
  • ❌ Forgetting that PCC stops at the aldehyde (doesn't go to acid).
  • ❌ Confusing Williamson direction: alkoxide + 1° R–X, not the reverse (avoid 3° R–X).
  • ❌ Mixing up Claisen (allyl aryl ether → allylphenol) and Fries (phenyl acetate → acylphenol).
  • ❌ Thinking that phenol is acidic enough to liberate CO2 from NaHCO3. It is not (weaker than H2CO3).
  • ❌ Using aq. KOH with 3° alkyl halide (E2 wins). Use alcoholic KOH for elimination, aqueous for substitution — on 1° halides.
  • ❌ Calling methanol a primary alcohol in the "resonance / delocalisation" sense — it is 1° because the C is sp³ and bonded to one alkyl group, but the methyl group provides no H-bonding/relevant stabilisation issues for the methoxide.

13. Final Take-Aways

1. Acidity ladder (most → least): Picric acid > o-/p-Nitrophenol > m-Nitrophenol > Phenol > Benzyl alcohol ≈ H2O > 1° alcohol > 2° alcohol > 3° alcohol > Ether (no O–H, so non-acidic).
2. Boiling-point ladder: Carboxylic acid > Alcohol ≈ Phenol (H-bonding) > Aldehyde/Ketone > Amine > Ether ≈ Alkane.
3. Oxidation ladder (easiest → hardest): Aldehyde > 1° alcohol > 2° alcohol. 3° alcohol: ✗.
4. For "A → B → C" identification problems: Track carbon count + functional group transformation. A 2° alcohol always gives a ketone on mild oxidation. A 1° alcohol always gives an aldehyde with PCC.
5. Phenol is the bridge between alcohols and arenes. It reacts with NaOH (like an acid), with electrophiles on the ring (like an arene), and with FeCl3 / Br2(aq.) (like itself — distinctive tests).
6. Ethers are the silent workhorses. Inert, polar, low-boiling solvents. The only "real" reactions are cleavage by HI/HBr and the Claisen rearrangement for aryl allyl ethers.
