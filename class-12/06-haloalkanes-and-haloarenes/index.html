Priority 1 (Essential for Mains & Advanced): - SN2: Primary halides + strong Nu (OH⁻, CN⁻, OR⁻) - SN1: Tertiary halides (e.g., t-BuBr) + weak Nu (H₂O, EtOH) - E2: 2° & 3° halides + alcoholic base (KOH, NaOH) - E1: 3° halides + aqueous weak base (H₂O, dilute acid) - Grignard formation & reactions

Priority 2 (Advanced/Synthesis-heavy): - Finkelstein reaction (Cl/Br → I via NaI) - Williamson ether synthesis (R-X + OR⁻) - Wurtz coupling (2 R-X + Na) - HVZ reaction (α-halogenation of carboxylic acids)

8.2: Must-Know Haloarene Reactions

Priority 1: - Sandmeyer: ArNH₂ → ArCl/ArBr/ArI via diazonium + Cu(I) - EAS: Br₂/FeBr₃, HNO₃/H₂SO₄, FC-Alkylation (with AlCl₃) on haloarenes (ortho/para) - SNAr with strong Nu (OH⁻, OR⁻, NH₂⁻) at high T

Priority 2: - Benzyne mechanism (NaNH₂ in liquid NH₃) - Coupling reactions (Suzuki, Stille, Ullmann) - Gattermann reaction (Cu powder + HX, milder Sandmeyer alternative)

Haloalkane SN2 Reactivity (with OH⁻)

Halogenation Reactivity Order (Nucleophilicity)

Haloarene Reactivity (SNAr)

Reactivity: F > Cl > Br > I (reverse of haloalkanes; EAS-driven)

EAS on Haloarenes: Directing Effects

  • Halogens = Ortho/Para Directors (deactivating)
  • Reduced reactivity vs. benzene; requires forcing conditions
  • Lone pairs on X (resonance donation) weak; -I effect dominates
