📋 Nomenclature & Isomerism

🧪 Werner's Coordination Theory (1893)

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected
Key Postulates:
  1. Metals exhibit two types of valencies: Primary (ionizable) and Secondary (non-ionizable)
  2. Primary valency = Oxidation state (satisfied by anions, shown by dotted lines)
  3. Secondary valency = Coordination number (satisfied by ligands/anions, shown by solid lines)
  4. Secondary valencies are directed towards fixed positions in space → definite geometry

📛 IUPAC Nomenclature Rules

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

Rules for Complex Ion:

  1. Name cation first, then anion
  2. List ligands alphabetically (ignore prefixes)
  3. Anionic ligands end in "-o": Cl⁻→chloro, CN⁻→cyano
  4. Neutral ligands: NH₃→ammine, H₂O→aqua, CO→carbonyl
  5. Prefixes: di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa
  6. If ligand name has prefix: use bis, tris, tetrakis
  7. Metal name: ends in "-ate" for anions
  8. Oxidation state in Roman numerals in parentheses

🔄 Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

A. Structural Isomerism

1. Linkage Isomerism

Same ligand binds through different donor atoms

[Co(NH₃)₅(NO₂)]²⁺

  • Nitro (yellow): bonds through N
  • Nitrito (red): bonds through O → [Co(NH₃)₅(ONO)]²⁺

Ambidentate ligands: NO₂⁻, SCN⁻, CN⁻

2. Coordination Isomerism

Exchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complex ions

[Co(NH₃)₆][Cr(CN)₆]

↔

[Cr(NH₃)₆][Co(CN)₆]

3. Ionization Isomerism

Same formula gives different ions in solution

[Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄

↔

[Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br

4. Solvate (Hydrate) Isomerism

Difference in whether solvent is inside coordination sphere or outside

[Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₃ (violet)

[Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O (blue-green)

[Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]Cl·2H₂O (dark green)

B. Stereoisomerism

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

1. Geometric (Cis-Trans) Isomerism

Occurs in: Square planar & Octahedral complexes

Not possible in: Tetrahedral complexes (all positions equivalent)

MA₄B₂ type:
  • Cis: Two B ligands adjacent (90°)
  • Trans: Two B ligands opposite (180°)
MA₃B₃ type:
  • Fac (facial): Three same ligands on one face
  • Mer (meridional): Three same ligands in a plane

2. Optical Isomerism

Occurs in: Complexes with no plane of symmetry

Types: d (dextro) and l (laevo) - non-superimposable mirror images

Common examples:
  • [Co(en)₃]³⁺
  • [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺ (cis only - trans is optically inactive)
  • [Cr(ox)₃]³⁻
  • Tetrahedral: [M(AA)₂X₂]
Key Point: Trans isomers generally have plane of symmetry → optically INACTIVE

🔗 Bonding Theories

🧬 Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected
Postulates:
  1. Central metal atom/ion provides empty orbitals for bonding
  2. Orbitals undergo hybridization
  3. Ligands donate electron pairs → coordinate bonds
  4. Geometry depends on type of hybridization
Coord. No. Hybridization Geometry Example Magnetic

6d²sp³Octahedral (inner)[Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺Diamagnetic
⚡ Board Exam Focus - Hybridization Calculation:
  1. Find oxidation state of metal
  2. Write d-electron configuration
  3. Strong field ligand (CN⁻, CO, NH₃) → pairing occurs → inner orbital complex
  4. Weak field ligand (F⁻, Cl⁻, H₂O) → no pairing → outer orbital complex

⚡ Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected
Core Concept: Electrostatic interaction between metal d-orbitals and ligand electrons. Ligands are treated as point charges that cause splitting of d-orbitals.

Octahedral Splitting (Δo)

Five degenerate d-orbitals split into:

  • t₂g (lower energy): dxy, dyz, dzx — point between axes
  • eg (higher energy): dx²-y², dz² — point along axes (more repulsion)

Tetrahedral Splitting (Δt)

Opposite pattern to octahedral:

  • e (lower energy): dx²-y², dz²
  • t₂ (higher energy): dxy, dyz, dzx

Δt = (4/9) Δo

⚡ Key Relationships:
  • If Δo < P (pairing energy) → High spin complex (weak field ligand)
  • If Δo > P → Low spin complex (strong field ligand)
  • CFSE = (-0.4x + 0.6y) Δo, where x = electrons in t2g, y = electrons in eg

🎨 Color & Magnetic Properties

Origin of Color

  • d-d transition: electron absorbs photon → jumps from t₂g to eg
  • Ephoton = hν = Δo
  • Complementary color observed
Colorless complexes:
  • d⁰ configuration (no d-d transition possible)
  • d¹⁰ configuration (no empty d-orbitals)
  • Example: [Sc(H₂O)₆]³⁺ (Sc³⁺ is d⁰)
  • Example: [Zn(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (Zn²⁺ is d¹⁰)

Magnetic Moment

μ = √(n(n+2)) BM (Bohr Magneton)

📝 NCERT Solutions - Coordination Compounds

Q 9.1: Indicate the types of isomerism exhibited by the following complexes: [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂, [Co(en)₃]Cl₃

Solution:

  1. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂: Shows ionization isomerism with [Co(NH₃)₅Cl₂]Cl. No geometric or optical isomerism as it's an MA₅B type complex.
  2. [Co(en)₃]Cl₃: Shows optical isomerism (d and l forms). The complex has no plane of symmetry and exists as non-superimposable mirror images.
Q 9.2: Give IUPAC names for: (i) [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ (ii) K₂[NiCl₄] (iii) [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl(NO₂)]

Solution:

  1. [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃: Hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride
    Oxidation state: x + 0 = +3, so Co is +3
  2. K₂[NiCl₄]: Potassium tetrachloridonickelate(II)
    Oxidation state: 2(+1) + x + 4(-1) = 0, so Ni is +2. Anion complex → "-ate" suffix
  3. [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl(NO₂)]: Diamminechloridonitrito-N-platinum(II)
    Oxidation state: x + 0 + (-1) + (-1) = 0, so Pt is +2. Ligands alphabetical: ammine, chlorido, nitrito-N
Q 9.3: Explain the bonding in [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ using VBT

Solution:

  1. Central metal: Fe²⁺ (atomic number 26)
  2. Electronic configuration of Fe: [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²
  3. Fe²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁶
  4. CN⁻ is a strong field ligand → causes pairing
  5. 3d orbitals: ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ (all paired)
  6. Hybridization: d²sp³ (uses two 3d, one 4s, three 4p orbitals)
  7. Geometry: Octahedral
  8. Magnetic property: Diamagnetic (no unpaired electrons)
Q 9.4: What is crystal field splitting energy? How does it determine configuration?

Solution:

Crystal Field Splitting Energy (Δo): The energy difference between the two sets of d-orbitals (t₂g and eg) in an octahedral field.

  • Δo < P (pairing energy): Electrons enter eg level → High spin complex (weak field ligands like F⁻, H₂O)
  • Δo > P: Electrons pair up in t₂g before entering eg → Low spin complex (strong field ligands like CN⁻, CO, NH₃)
Example: For d⁶ configuration:
  • Weak field: t₂g⁴ eg² (4 unpaired e⁻)
  • Strong field: t₂g⁶ (0 unpaired e⁻)
Q 9.5: Predict the number of unpaired electrons in [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ and [Cr(CN)₆]³⁻

Solution:

Central metal: Cr³⁺ (atomic number 24)

Electronic configuration: Cr³⁺ is [Ar] 3d³

  1. [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺: H₂O is weak field ligand
    3d³ configuration: t₂g³ eg⁰
    Unpaired electrons = 3
  2. [Cr(CN)₆]³⁻: CN⁻ is strong field ligand
    But d³ configuration has only 3 electrons, so even with strong field, they all go to t₂g
    Unpaired electrons = 3
Note: For d¹, d², d³ configurations, the number of unpaired electrons is the same regardless of ligand strength because all electrons fit in the lower t₂g level.
Q 9.6: Draw all geometrical isomers of [Pt(NH₃)(Br)(Cl)(py)]

Solution:

This is a square planar complex of type [Mabcd] with four different ligands.

For [Mabcd] type, there are 3 geometrical isomers:

  1. Isomer 1: NH₃ and Br trans to each other, Cl and py trans to each other
  2. Isomer 2: NH₃ and Cl trans to each other, Br and py trans to each other
  3. Isomer 3: NH₃ and py trans to each other, Br and Cl trans to each other
Formula: Number of geometrical isomers for [Mabcd] = 3
Q 9.7: How many geometrical isomers are possible for [Co(en)₃]³⁺?

Solution:

[Co(en)₃]³⁺ is an octahedral complex with three bidentate ethylenediamine ligands.

  • Since all three ligands are identical (en), no geometrical isomerism is possible
  • However, the complex has optical isomerism (d and l forms)
  • Total isomers = 2 (optical isomers only)
Key Point: Complexes of type [M(AA)₃] where AA = symmetric bidentate ligand show only optical isomerism, not geometric isomerism.
Q 9.8: Why is [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ diamagnetic while [NiCl₄]²⁻ is paramagnetic?

Solution:

Central metal: Ni²⁺ (atomic number 28)

Electronic configuration: Ni²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁸

  1. [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻:
    • CN⁻ is a strong field ligand
    • Causes pairing of 3d electrons
    • Hybridization: dsp² (square planar)
    • All electrons paired → Diamagnetic
  2. [NiCl₄]²⁻:
    • Cl⁻ is a weak field ligand
    • No pairing of 3d electrons
    • Hybridization: sp³ (tetrahedral)
    • 2 unpaired electrons → Paramagnetic
Q 9.9: Explain why [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ is strongly paramagnetic while [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻ is weakly paramagnetic

Solution:

Central metal: Fe³⁺ (d⁵ configuration)

  1. [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺:
    • H₂O is weak field ligand → Δo < P
    • High spin complex: t₂g³ eg²
    • 5 unpaired electrons → Strongly paramagnetic
    • μ = √(5×7) = 5.92 BM
  2. [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻:
    • CN⁻ is strong field ligand → Δo > P
    • Low spin complex: t₂g⁵
    • 1 unpaired electron → Weakly paramagnetic
    • μ = √(1×3) = 1.73 BM
Q 9.10: Give evidence that [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ and [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ are ionization isomers

Solution:

Ionization isomers give different ions in solution, which can be detected by precipitation reactions.

  1. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ in water:
    → [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺ + 2Cl⁻
    Add AgNO₃: White ppt of AgCl (from Cl⁻ ions)
    Add BaCl₂: No ppt (no SO₄²⁻ ions)
  2. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]SO₄ in water:
    → [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]⁺ + SO₄²⁻
    Add AgNO₃: No ppt (no free Cl⁻ ions)
    Add BaCl₂: White ppt of BaSO₄
Conclusion: The different precipitation reactions prove these are ionization isomers.
Q 9.11: Calculate CFSE for [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ given Δo = 23000 cm⁻¹

Solution:

  1. Co³⁺ is d⁶ configuration
  2. NH₃ is strong field ligand → Low spin complex
  3. Configuration: t₂g⁶ eg⁰
  4. CFSE = (-0.4 × nt2g + 0.6 × neg) × Δo
  5. CFSE = (-0.4 × 6 + 0.6 × 0) × 23000
  6. CFSE = -2.4 × 23000 = -55200 cm⁻¹
Note: Negative CFSE indicates stabilization. The higher the magnitude, the more stable the complex.
Q 9.12: Why is [Sc(H₂O)₆]³⁺ colorless?

Solution:

  1. Sc³⁺ has electronic configuration: [Ar] 3d⁰
  2. There are no d-electrons in Sc³⁺
  3. For color to appear, d-d transition must occur (electron jumps from t₂g to eg)
  4. Since there are no d-electrons, no d-d transition is possible
  5. Therefore, no light is absorbed in visible region
  6. Result: The complex appears colorless
General Rule: Complexes with d⁰ or d¹⁰ configurations are colorless because d-d transitions are not possible.
Q 9.13: Predict magnetic moment of [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺

Solution:

  1. Ni²⁺ is d⁸ configuration
  2. H₂O is weak field ligand
  3. In octahedral field: t₂g⁶ eg²
  4. Number of unpaired electrons (n) = 2
  5. μ = √(n(n+2)) = √(2×4) = √8 = 2.83 BM

Q 9.15: What is the spectrochemical series? Give examples.

Solution:

Spectrochemical Series: An ordering of ligands based on the magnitude of crystal field splitting they cause.

I⁻ < Br⁻ < S²⁻ < SCN⁻ < Cl⁻ < N₃⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < H₂O < NCS⁻ < NH₃ < en < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ < CO
  • Weak field ligands (left side): I⁻, Br⁻, Cl⁻, F⁻, H₂O → Small Δo, high spin complexes
  • Strong field ligands (right side): NH₃, en, CN⁻, CO → Large Δo, low spin complexes
Application: Predicting magnetic behavior and color of complexes based on ligand position in the series.
