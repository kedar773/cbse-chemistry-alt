Carbohydrates ⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones, or compounds that yield these on hydrolysis. General formula: Cn(H2O)n — hence "hydrates of carbon".

1. Classification of Carbohydrates

🟢 Monosaccharides

Cannot be hydrolyzed further

  • Trioses (Glyceraldehyde, DHAP)
  • Tetroses (Erythrose)
  • Pentoses (Ribose, Ribulose)
  • Hexoses (Glucose, Fructose, Galactose) ⚡
  • Heptoses (Sedoheptulose)

🔗 Disaccharides

Two monosaccharide units (glycosidic linkage)

  • Maltose (α-1,4) — Glucose + Glucose
  • Lactose (β-1,4) — Galactose + Glucose
  • Sucrose (α-1,2β) — Glucose + Fructose

📊 Oligosaccharides

2–10 monosaccharide units

  • Raffinose (trisaccharide)
  • Stachyose (tetrasaccharide)

🌾 Polysaccharides

Many monosaccharide units (homopolymers/heteropolymers)

  • Starch: Amylose + Amylopectin (storage)
  • Glycogen: Animal storage (branched)
  • Cellulose: β-1,4 structural (plant cell wall)
  • Chitin, Pectin, Hemicellulose
📌 Classification by Functional Group: Aldoses (containing -CHO, e.g., Glucose, Galactose) and Ketoses (containing >C=O, e.g., Fructose, Ribulose).
📌 By D/L Configuration: D-sugars (–OH on right at lowest chiral C in Fischer) vs L-sugars (–OH on left). Most natural sugars are D-configured.

2. Monosaccharides — Glucose & Fructose

D-Glucose (Dextrose, Grape Sugar, Blood Sugar) Most abundant monosaccharide

Molecular formula: C6H12O6 | Aldohexose | [α]D20 = +52.7° (dextrorotatory)

Open-Chain (Fischer) Projection

Cyclic (Haworth) Structure — Pyranose Form

🔁 Mutarotation: When α-D-glucose (or β-D-glucose) is dissolved in water, the optical rotation changes until equilibrium is reached at +52.5°. This happens via the open-chain form. The α and β anomers interconvert through the open-chain aldehyde form.

D-Fructose (Levulose, Fruit Sugar) ⚡ PYQ

Molecular formula: C6H12O6 | Ketohexose | [α]D20 = −92.0° (laevorotatory — note the inversion!)

Fructose (Fischer):
CH2OH
|    ↑ (C=O at C-2)
CH2OH—C(=O)—CH(OH)—CH(OH)—CH(OH)—CH2OH
📌 Furanose Form: Fructose forms a 5-membered (furanose) ring when C-5 —OH attacks C-2 carbonyl. Both α-D-fructofuranose and β-D-fructofuranose exist.

3. Reactions Confirming the Structure of Glucose ⚡ CBSE PYQ - High Yield

🔬 Five-Proof Structural Analysis of D-Glucose

The straight-chain structure of glucose is established through the following sequence of reactions:

1
Elemental Analysis & Molecular Formula:
C : H : O ratio = 40.0% : 6.67% : 53.33%
Empirical formula = CH2O | Molecular mass = 180 g/mol
⇒ Molecular formula: C6H12O6
2
Straight-Chain Proof (Reduction with HI/Red P):
C6H12O6 + 6HI —(Δ)→ n-hexane (C6H14) + 6I2 + 6H2O
All 6 carbons linked in a single straight chain. ⚡
3
Carbonyl Group Proof (Hydroxylamine):
C6H12O6 + NH2OH → Glucose oxime + H2O
Reacts with 1 mol NH2OH → contains one >C=O group (carbonyl).
4
Nature of Carbonyl — Aldehyde (Tollen's/Fehling's Test):
C6H12O6 + 2[Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3OH− → C6H11O7− + 2Ag↓ + 4NH3 + 2H2O
Gives silver mirror with Tollen's reagent & brick-red Cu2O with Fehling's → —CHO group (aldehyde). ⚡
5
Primary Alcohol Proof (HNO3 Oxidation):
CH2OH—(CHOH)4—CHO + [O] (HNO3) → HOOC—(CHOH)4—COOH
Forms a dicarboxylic acid (saccharic acid) → 1° alcohol at C-6. ⚡
6
Number of —OH Groups (Acetic Anhydride):
C6H7O(OH)5 + 5(CH3CO)2O → C6H7O(OCOCH3)5 + 5CH3COOH
Forms pentaacetate with acetic anhydride/pyridine → 5 —OH groups (4 secondary + 1 primary).
7
Cyclic α/β Anomer Proof (Mutarotation):
α-D-glucose [α]D = +112° → on standing in H2O → equilibrium value +52.5° (β-anomer +18.7°).
This rotation change is only possible via the cyclic hemiacetal form. ⚡

4. Disaccharides — Glycosidic Linkages ⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

Disaccharides are formed by condensation of two monosaccharide units with elimination of one H2O molecule and formation of a glycosidic (C—O—C) linkage.

DisaccharideConstituent UnitsLinkageReducing?Source
Maltose α-D-Glucose + α-D-Glucose α-1,4 glycosidic ✅ Yes (free —CHO at C-1) Starch hydrolysis
Lactose β-D-Galactose + D-Glucose β-1,4 glycosidic ✅ Yes (free —CHO at C-1 of glucose) Milk sugar
Sucrose α-D-Glucose + β-D-Fructose α-1,2β glycosidic (C-1 of glucose + C-2 of fructose) ❌ No (both anomeric C locked) Cane sugar
📌 Why is Sucrose Non-Reducing? In sucrose, the C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose are BOTH involved in the glycosidic linkage. This means there is no free —CHO or —C—OH (anomeric carbon) to open up and reduce Tollen's/Fehling's reagent. Upon hydrolysis, sucrose yields an equimolar mixture of glucose + fructose called invert sugar (because hydrolysis inverts rotation from dextrorotatory to laevorotatory). ⚡

Proteins, Vitamins & Nucleic Acids

Proteins are the most versatile biomacromolecules in living systems. They are polymers of α-amino acids linked by peptide bonds.

1. α-Amino Acids ⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

⚛️

General Structure

General formula of α-amino acid:

    H
    |
R — C — COOH
    |
    NH2

Glycine: R = H | Alanine: R = CH3 | Serine: R = CH2OH

Out of ~300 natural amino acids, only 20 α-amino acids are encoded by the genetic code and incorporated into proteins (standard amino acids). All have L-configuration in proteins (except glycine, which is achiral).

Classification of Amino Acids

Based on R-group (side chain)

  • Non-polar (hydrophobic): Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Pro, Phe, Trp, Met, Gly
  • Polar (hydrophilic): Ser, Thr, Cys, Tyr, Asn, Gln
  • Positively charged (basic): Lys, Arg, His
  • Negatively charged (acidic): Asp, Glu

Based on nutritional requirement ⚡

  • Essential (10): Val, Leu, Ile, Phe, Trp, Met, Thr, Lys, Arg*, His*
  • Non-essential: Gly, Ala, Ser, Cys, Tyr, Asn, Gln, Asp, Glu, Pro

*Semi-essential: required during growth/illness

Zwitterion (Dipolar Ion) ⚡ PYQ

In aqueous solution at physiological pH (~7.4), amino acids exist predominantly as zwitterions (dipolar ions):

    H        O
    |        ‖
R — C — C — O−
    |        
    +H3N

Zwitterion (net charge = 0, but has + and − charges)

The isoelectric point (pI) is the pH at which the amino acid has zero net charge. At pH < pI → cation dominates; at pH > pI → anion dominates. Zwitterions explain:

  • High melting points (ionic character)
  • High dipole moment
  • Solubility in polar solvents, insolubility in non-polar
  • Buffered behavior (amphoteric: H+ donor and acceptor)

2. Peptide Bond & Protein Structure ⚡ CBSE PYQ - High Yield

Peptide Linkage —CO—NH—

Formed by condensation of —COOH of one amino acid with —NH2 of another, eliminating one H2O molecule. Peptides: dipeptide (2), tripeptide (3), oligopeptide (≤10), polypeptide (>10).

H2N—CHR1—CO—OH + H—NH—CHR2—COOH → H2N—CHR1—CO—NH—CHR2—COOH + H2O

📌 The peptide bond is planar (partial double-bond character, ~40% double-bond, due to resonance between C=O and C=N). This restricts rotation and stabilizes the backbone. 📌 [See Tab 4 Simulator 2 for animation]

Denaturation of Proteins ⚡

Loss of 2°, 3°, and 4° structure (primary structure intact) due to disruption of H-bonds, disulfide bonds, ionic interactions. Caused by:

  • Heat (egg albumin coagulation, milk curdling)
  • pH change (acid/alkali)
  • Heavy metal ions (Hg2+, Pb2+ — bind to —SH groups)
  • Organic solvents (ethanol, acetone — disrupt hydrophobic interactions)
  • Urea (competes for H-bonds), radiation (X-rays, UV)

📌 Examples: Curdling of milk (acid), coagulation of egg white (heat), salting out of soap, biological activity loss of enzymes at high T.

Three Components of a Nucleotide

  • Purines (2 rings): Adenine (A), Guanine (G)

📌 In-Text Questions

14.1Glucose or sucrose are soluble in water but cyclohexane or benzene (simple six membered ring compounds) are insoluble in water. Explain. 2 marks

Concept: Hydrogen bonding and polarity determine water solubility.

Answer: Glucose and sucrose contain multiple —OH groups capable of forming extensive hydrogen bonds with water molecules. As a result, they dissolve readily in water.

In contrast, cyclohexane and benzene are non-polar hydrocarbons with no polar functional groups. They cannot form hydrogen bonds with water, so they remain insoluble. (The 'like dissolves like' rule.)

14.2What are the expected products of hydrolysis of lactose? 1 mark

Answer: Lactose is a disaccharide composed of β-D-galactose and D-glucose linked by a β-1,4 glycosidic bond.

On hydrolysis (acid or enzyme lactase):

Lactose + H2O —(HCl/lactase)→ β-D-Galactose + D-Glucose

📌 See Tab 1: Disaccharide structures ⚡ PYQ 2022

14.3How do you explain the absence of aldehyde group in the pentaacetate of D-glucose? 2 marks

Concept: Cyclic hemiacetal structure of glucose.

Answer: D-Glucose exists predominantly in the cyclic (pyranose) form, in which the C-1 aldehyde has reacted with the C-5 hydroxyl to form an intramolecular hemiacetal. The —CHO group is no longer free in the cyclic structure.

When treated with acetic anhydride, all five —OH groups (including the C-1 hemiacetal —OH, which exists in equilibrium with the open-chain form) get acetylated to form pentaacetate. The C-1 —OH of the hemiacetal is what gets acetylated; hence no free —CHO remains in pentaacetate.

This proves the cyclic structure of glucose. ⚡ PYQ 2020

📚 Exercise Questions (NCERT)

14.4Explain the term 'invert sugar'. What is the significance of this name? 2 marks

Answer: Invert sugar is the equimolar mixture of D-glucose and D-fructose obtained by hydrolysis of sucrose.

Sucrose + H2O —(H+ or invertase)→ D-Glucose + D-Fructose

Significance of the name: Sucrose is dextrorotatory with [α]D = +66.5°. The hydrolysis products are D-glucose (+52.7°) and D-fructose (−92°). The mixture has net [α]D = (−92 + 52.7)/2 ≈ −20° (laevorotatory). The sign of rotation has been "inverted" from + to −, hence the name. The enzyme that catalyses this is called invertase.

14.7Explain why sucrose is a non-reducing sugar while glucose and fructose are reducing sugars. 3 marks

Concept: Reducing sugars have a free anomeric carbon (free —CHO or capable of forming one via ring opening).

Glucose (reducing): In its cyclic pyranose form, the C-1 anomeric carbon has a free —OH that can open to give the free —CHO group. The free —CHO reduces Tollen's reagent (Ag+ → Ag) and Fehling's solution (Cu2+ → Cu2O).

Fructose (reducing): Although it is a ketose, in alkaline medium it undergoes tautomerization (Lobry de Bruyn transformation) via the enediol intermediate to give a mixture of glucose and mannose, both of which are aldoses. These reduce Tollen's/Fehling's.

Sucrose (non-reducing): The glycosidic linkage is formed between C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose. Both anomeric carbons are involved in the linkage, so neither is free to open up. Hence sucrose cannot reduce Tollen's or Fehling's. ⚡

14.8What are the two specific tests for glucose? What do they indicate? 2 marks

Test 1: Tollen's Test (Silver Mirror Test):

  • Reagent: Ammoniacal silver nitrate [Ag(NH3)2]+
  • Observation: Bright silver mirror forms on the test tube wall
  • Indicates: Presence of free —CHO group (aldehyde)

Test 2: Fehling's Test:

  • Reagent: Fehling's A (CuSO4) + Fehling's B (alkaline Rochelle salt)
  • Observation: Brick-red precipitate of Cu2O
  • Indicates: Presence of free —CHO group

Both confirm glucose is a reducing aldose. 📌 See Tab 4 Simulator 1: Tollen's Test

14.9Enumerate the reactions of D-glucose which cannot be explained by its straight-chain structure. 3 marks

Reactions that suggest cyclic structure:

  1. Pentaacetate formation: Glucose forms a pentaacetate with acetic anhydride (5 —OH groups), but the pentaacetate does not react with hydroxylamine or 2,4-DNP — meaning the —CHO has been lost. This can only be explained by cyclic structure where C-1 is part of a hemiacetal.
  2. Mutarotation: α-D-Glucose [α]D = +112° and β-D-Glucose [α]D = +18.7° both change to +52.5° in aqueous solution. This is only possible if both anomers interconvert via the open-chain form (cyclic ⇌ open chain ⇌ other anomer).
  3. Two different crystalline forms: α-D-glucose crystallizes from concentrated solution at room temperature, β-D-glucose from hot saturated solution. Two distinct anomers exist.
  4. Reaction with methanol/HCl gives two methyl glucosides (α and β methyl glucoside) — the anomeric C-1 —OH can be methylated in two ways.

14.11What are the essential and non-essential amino acids? Give two examples of each. 2 marks

14.12Define the following terms: (a) Glycosidic linkage (b) Peptide linkage (c) Primary structure (d) Denaturation 4 marks

(a) Glycosidic linkage: The —C—O—C— bond formed between two monosaccharide units by condensation (loss of H2O), linking C-1 (anomeric) of one sugar to a hydroxyl of another.

(b) Peptide linkage: The —CO—NH— amide bond formed between the —COOH of one α-amino acid and the —NH2 of another with elimination of H2O. It is planar with partial double bond character.

(c) Primary structure: The specific linear sequence of amino acids linked by peptide bonds in a polypeptide chain. The primary structure determines all higher levels of organization.

(d) Denaturation: Loss of native 2°, 3°, and 4° structure of a protein (primary structure remains intact) due to disruption of H-bonds, disulfide bonds, etc. by heat, pH, heavy metals, or organic solvents. Usually irreversible.

⚡ PYQ - Frequently asked

14.14Explain the terms: (a) Co-enzyme (b) Prosthetic group (c) Co-factors 3 marks

(a) Co-enzyme: Small, non-protein, organic molecules (often derived from vitamins) that are loosely bound to the enzyme and essential for catalytic activity. Examples: NAD+, NADP+, FAD, coenzyme A, thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP). They act as carriers of specific atoms or functional groups.

(b) Prosthetic group: The non-protein organic group that is tightly and permanently bound to the enzyme (often covalently). Examples: Heme in haemoglobin/catalase, FAD in succinate dehydrogenase, biotin in carboxylases.

(c) Co-factors: Non-protein components (metal ions or organic molecules) required for enzyme activity. They can be subdivided into:

  • Inorganic cofactors: Metal ions like Zn2+ (in carbonic anhydrase), Mg2+ (in kinases), Fe2+/Fe3+ (in cytochromes), Cu2+ (in cytochrome oxidase)
  • Organic cofactors (coenzymes): NAD+, FAD, CoA, etc.

An apoenzyme (protein part) + cofactor = holoenzyme (active form).

14.15What is the difference between DNA and RNA? 3 marks

Refer to comparison table in Tab 2. Key differences:

  • Sugar: DNA has 2-deoxyribose; RNA has ribose (with 2'-OH)
  • Bases: DNA has thymine (T); RNA has uracil (U) in place of T
  • Strands: DNA is double-stranded (double helix); RNA is single-stranded
  • Function: DNA stores genetic information; RNA helps in protein synthesis (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA)
  • Location: DNA is mainly in nucleus; RNA is mainly in cytoplasm
  • Replication: DNA can self-replicate; RNA is synthesized from DNA as needed
  • Base pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds) in DNA; A=U (2 H-bonds) in RNA; G≡C in both (3 H-bonds)

14.17Name the two secondary structures of proteins. 1 mark

The two main types of secondary structure of proteins are:

  1. α-Helix (right-handed coiled rod, 3.6 residues per turn, i → i+4 H-bonds)
  2. β-Pleated Sheet (extended polypeptide strands, parallel or antiparallel, inter-strand H-bonds)

Both are stabilized by hydrogen bonds between the N-H and C=O groups of the peptide backbone.

14.20Explain the mechanism of enzyme action. 3 marks

Two models of enzyme-substrate interaction:

(1) Lock and Key Model (Emil Fischer, 1894): The enzyme has a rigid, pre-formed active site whose shape is exactly complementary to the substrate. Only the correct substrate (key) fits into the active site (lock).

(2) Induced Fit Model (Daniel Koshland, 1958): The active site is not rigid. When the substrate approaches, the enzyme changes shape to wrap around and bind the substrate optimally. The substrate induces the conformational change.

Mechanism (Michaelis–Menten):

E + S ⇌ [ES] → E + P
  1. Substrate (S) binds to enzyme's active site → Enzyme-Substrate complex [ES]
  2. Activation energy is lowered (transition state stabilized)
  3. [ES] is converted to Enzyme-Product complex [EP]
  4. Product (P) is released; enzyme is regenerated unchanged

The enzyme is highly specific, works in mild conditions, and dramatically increases reaction rate (often 106–1012 fold).

🎯 CBSE Previous Year Questions (PYQ)

PYQ-1Assertion-Reason: (A) Glucose exists in two different crystalline forms α-D and β-D. (R) The two forms differ in configuration at C-1 (anomeric carbon). 1 mark

Answer: Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

α-D-glucose and β-D-glucose are anomers — they differ only in the configuration at the anomeric carbon C-1 (in the cyclic pyranose form). α has —OH down (axial), β has —OH up (equatorial) at C-1.

PYQ-2Case-based: A biochemist isolates a disaccharide from milk. Upon hydrolysis with dilute acid, it gives two monosaccharides A and B. A is a ketose and B is an aldose. The disaccharide is non-reducing. (a) Identify the disaccharide. (b) Give the structure of A and B. (c) What is the linkage between A and B? (d) Why is it non-reducing? 5 marks

(a) Identification: From milk + ketose (fructose) + aldose (glucose) + non-reducing → the disaccharide is sucrose. (Note: Lactose is also from milk but is reducing. The case mixes clues — answer is sucrose with the "from milk" being a distraction.)

(b) Structures: A = β-D-Fructose (furanose form), B = α-D-Glucose (pyranose form)

(c) Linkage: α-1,2β glycosidic linkage — between C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose

(d) Non-reducing because: Both anomeric carbons (C-1 of glucose and C-2 of fructose) are involved in the glycosidic bond, so neither can open up to provide a free —CHO or —C(OH)= group to reduce Tollen's/Fehling's reagent.

PYQ-3Explain why (a) Cellulose is not digested by humans (b) Starch gives blue color with iodine (c) Glycine is optically inactive. 3 marks

(a) Cellulose: Humans lack the enzyme cellulase that can hydrolyze the β-1,4 glycosidic linkages in cellulose. Herbivores have symbiotic gut bacteria that produce cellulase.

(b) Starch-iodine test: I2 molecules fit into the helical coil of amylose (linear α-1,4 polymer), forming a charge-transfer complex that absorbs visible light in the 620 nm region, appearing deep blue. Amylopectin gives blue-violet; glycogen gives red-brown.

(c) Glycine: Glycine has R = H at the α-carbon, so it has no chiral (asymmetric) carbon. With only two substituents (H, H) at C-α, glycine is achiral and therefore optically inactive.

PYQ-4State whether the following statements are True or False. Justify: (a) All amino acids are optically active. (b) Cellulose contains β-1,4 glycosidic linkages. (c) In DNA, A pairs with G. (d) Vitamin C is water-soluble. 4 marks

(a) FALSE: Glycine (R = H) is the only standard amino acid that is not optically active because its α-carbon is not chiral (two H substituents).

(b) TRUE: Cellulose is a linear polymer of β-D-glucose linked by β-1,4 glycosidic bonds.

(c) FALSE: In DNA, adenine (A) pairs with thymine (T) via 2 hydrogen bonds; guanine (G) pairs with cytosine (C) via 3 hydrogen bonds.

(d) TRUE: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is a water-soluble vitamin, along with B-complex vitamins.
