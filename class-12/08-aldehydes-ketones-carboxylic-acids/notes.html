1. Nomenclature of Aldehydes & Ketones

General Formulas: Aldehydes: R–CHO; Ketones: R–CO–R′

  • Aldehydes: Replace "-e" with "-al". –CHO carbon is position 1.
  • Ketones: Replace "-e" with "-one". Lowest number for carbonyl.

3. Physical Properties (Aldehydes & Ketones)

  • Boiling points: Lower than alcohols (no H-bonding), higher than ethers/alkanes.
  • Solubility: C1-C4 soluble in water (H-bond with H2O).
  • Methanal is gas; ethanal is volatile liquid.
📌 [In-Text Q 8.5] Boiling point order: butane < ethoxyethane < propanal < propan-1-ol.

Preparation of Aldehydes & Ketones · Tab 2

2.1 Oxidation of Alcohols

1° alcohol + PCC → Aldehyde. 2° alcohol + K2Cr2O7/H+ → Ketone.

📌 [Example 8.1] Butan-2-ol → butan-2-one.

2.2 Dehydrogenation (Cu, 573 K)

RCH2OH → RCHO + H2 (1° alcohol → aldehyde)

2.3 Ozonolysis of Alkenes

RCH=CHR' + O3 → Zn/H2O → RCHO + R'CHO

📌 [In-Text Q 8.2] 2-methylbut-2-ene → propan-2-one + ethanal.

2.4 Hydration of Alkynes

HC≡CH + H2O →HgSO4/H2SO4 CH3CHO

2.5 Rosenmund Reduction (RCOCl → RCHO)

H2/Pd-BaSO4 (poisoned catalyst).

📌 [Example 8.3] Ethanoyl chloride → ethanal.

2.6 Stephen Reaction (RCN → RCHO)

SnCl2/HCl then H2O.

2.7 Grignard + Nitrile → Ketone

RCN + R'MgX → H3O+ → R–CO–R'

2.8 Friedel–Crafts Acylation

Benzene + CH3COCl →AlCl3 C6H5COCH3

2.9 Gattermann–Koch Reaction (Benzene → Benzaldehyde)

CO + HCl + C6H6 →AlCl3/CuCl C6H5CHO

2.10 Etard Reaction (Toluene → Benzaldehyde)

CrO2Cl2/CS2 then H2O.

Reactions of Aldehydes & Ketones · Tab 3

🔬 Nucleophilic Addition (AN) Mechanism

Nu− attacks δ+ carbon → tetrahedral intermediate → protonation.

4.1 Addition of HCN

CH3CHO + HCN → CH3CH(OH)CN (cyanohydrin).

4.2 Addition of NaHSO3

Forms crystalline bisulphite adduct (reversible, purification).

4.3 Addition of Grignard Reagents

HCHO → 1° alcohol; other aldehydes → 2°; ketones → 3° alcohol.

📌 [Example 8.5] Propanone + CH3MgBr → (CH3)3COH.

4.4 Addition of Alcohols (Acetal/Ketal)

CH3CHO + 2 C2H5OH →dry HCl CH3CH(OC2H5)2

4.5 Ammonia Derivatives (Imine formation)

ReagentProduct
NH2OHOxime
NH2NH2Hydrazone
C6H5NHNH2Phenylhydrazone
2,4-DNPYellow/orange ppt (test)
📌 [In-Text Q 8.6] Semicarbazone of propanone: (CH3)2C=NNHCONH2.

4.6 Reduction

Catalytic / NaBH4: Aldehydes → 1° alcohol; Ketones → 2° alcohol.
Clemmensen: Zn-Hg / conc. HCl (acidic) → CH2.
Wolff–Kishner: NH2NH2 / KOH / glycol (basic) → CH2.

📌 [Example 8.6] Acetophenone → ethylbenzene (Clemmensen or Wolff–Kishner).

4.7 Oxidation

Tollens' test: Ag mirror (aldehydes).
Fehling's test: Cu2O red ppt (aliphatic aldehydes). Aromatic aldehydes do not react.
Iodoform test: CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– give yellow CHI3.

📌 [In-Text Q 8.8] Propan-2-ol & butan-2-one give iodoform; propanal, butan-1-ol do not.

4.8 Aldol Condensation (α-H required)

2 CH3CHO →dil. NaOH CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO →Δ CH3CH=CHCHO

📌 [Example 8.7] Propanal aldol → CH3CH2CH=C(CH3)CHO.

4.9 Cannizzaro Reaction (no α-H)

2 C6H5CHO + conc. NaOH → C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COONa

📌 [In-Text Q 8.9] Benzaldehyde does NOT give Fehling's test but gives Tollens'.

4.10 Electrophilic Substitution (meta-directing)

Nitration of benzaldehyde → m-nitrobenzaldehyde.

Master Study Notes

Carboxylic Acids · Tab 4

5. Nomenclature & Preparation

Replace "-e" with "-oic acid". HCOOH → methanoic acid; CH3COOH → ethanoic acid.

Preparation Methods

  • Oxidation of 1° alcohols / aldehydes (KMnO4/H+)
  • Hydrolysis of nitriles (RCN → RCOOH)
  • Carbonation of Grignard reagent: RMgX + CO2 → RCOOH
  • Hydrolysis of esters, acid chlorides, anhydrides.
📌 [Example 8.9] CH3Br → CH3MgBr →CO2/H+ CH3COOH.

6. Physical Properties & Acidity

Higher b.p. due to dimeric H‑bonding. Acidity: RCOOH ⇌ RCOO− + H+. EWG increases acidity; EDG decreases it.

📌 [Exercise 8.13] Acidity: 4-methoxybenzoic < 4-methylbenzoic < benzoic < 4-nitrobenzoic.

7. Chemical Reactions

7.1 Salt Formation

CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CO2↑ (test for –COOH)

7.2 Acid Chlorides

SOCl2 preferred (gaseous by‑products).

📌 [In-Text Q 8.10] SOCl2 gives pure RCOCl.

7.3 Esterification (Fischer)

RCOOH + R'OH ⇌H+ RCOOR' + H2O

7.4 Amide formation

RCOOH + NH3 → RCOONH4 →Δ RCONH2

7.5 Reduction

LiAlH4 or B2H6 → 1° alcohol.

7.6 Decarboxylation

RCOONa + NaOH/CaO → R–H + Na2CO3

7.7 Kolbe's Electrolysis

2 CH3COONa → CH3–CH3

7.8 HVZ Reaction (α‑halogenation)

RCH2COOH + Cl2/red P → RCHClCOOH

📌 [Example 8.10] Ethanoic acid → α‑chloroethanoic acid (HVZ).

In-Text Questions

📌 In-Text Q 8.2: Ozonolysis of 2-methylbut-2-ene

Answer: Propan-2-one (CH3COCH3) and ethanal (CH3CHO).

💡 Hint: Unsymmetrical alkene gives two carbonyl compounds.
📌 In-Text Q 8.5: Arrange boiling points: propan-1-ol, propanal, butane, ethoxyethane

Answer: Butane < Ethoxyethane < Propanal < Propan-1-ol.

💡 Hint: H-bonding > dipole-dipole > van der Waals.
📌 In-Text Q 8.6: Semicarbazone of propanone

Answer: (CH3)2C=NNHCONH2

💡 Hint: Replace =O with =N–NHCONH2.
📌 In-Text Q 8.8: Iodoform test – which give positive?

Answer: Propan-2-ol (CH3CHOHCH3) and butan-2-one (CH3COCH2CH3). Propanal and butan-1-ol give negative.

💡 Hint: CH3CO– or CH3CH(OH)– group required.
📌 In-Text Q 8.9: Benzaldehyde – Fehling's test?

Answer: No, benzaldehyde does not reduce Fehling's solution. It gives positive Tollens' test.

💡 Hint: Aromatic aldehydes resist oxidation under Fehling's alkaline conditions.
📌 In-Text Q 8.10: Why SOCl2 preferred over PCl5?

Answer: By‑products (SO2 + HCl) are gases, leaving pure acid chloride. PCl5 leaves POCl3 (liquid).

💡 Hint: Easier purification.

Exercise Questions

📌 Q 8.1: Structures of (i) 2-chloro-3-methylpentanal (ii) 3-bromo-4-phenylpentanoic acid (iii) hex-2-en-4-ynoic acid

Answer: (i) CH3CH2CH(CH3)CHClCHO
(ii) CH3CH(C6H5)CHBrCH2COOH
(iii) CH3C≡C–CH=CH–COOH

💡 Hint: Number chains correctly; –CHO and –COOH are terminal.
📌 Q 8.5: Predict products (i) propanone + HCN (ii) ethanal + NH2OH (iii) benzaldehyde + conc. NaOH

Answer: (i) (CH3)2C(OH)CN (cyanohydrin)
(ii) CH3CH=NOH (oxime)
(iii) C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COONa (Cannizzaro).

💡 Hint: Benzaldehyde has no α‑H → Cannizzaro.
📌 Q 8.7: Conversions (i) ethanal → but-2-enal (ii) propanone → 2-methylpropan-2-ol (iii) benzoic acid → benzaldehyde

Answer: (i) Aldol condensation: 2CH3CHO →dil. NaOH aldol →Δ CH3CH=CHCHO.
(ii) CH3COCH3 + CH3MgBr →H3O+ (CH3)3COH.
(iii) C6H5COOH →SOCl2 C6H5COCl →H2/Pd-BaSO4 C6H5CHO.

💡 Hint: (i) aldol, (ii) Grignard, (iii) Rosenmund.
📌 Q 8.9: Distinguish between (i) propanal & propanone (ii) benzaldehyde & acetophenone (iii) ethanal & propanal

Answer: (i) Tollens' test: propanal → Ag mirror; propanone → no reaction.
(ii) Iodoform test: acetophenone (CH3CO–) → yellow ppt; benzaldehyde → no reaction.
(iii) Iodoform test: ethanal (CH3CHO) → yellow ppt; propanal → no reaction.

💡 Hint: Aldehydes reduce Tollens'; methyl ketones give iodoform.
📌 Q 8.11: Boiling point of ethanoic acid higher than propan-1-ol?

Answer: Ethanoic acid forms strong dimers via H‑bonding, effectively doubling molecular mass.

💡 Hint: Dimerisation in carboxylic acids.
📌 Q 8.13: Acidity order: benzoic, 4-nitrobenzoic, 4-methoxybenzoic, 4-methylbenzoic

Answer: 4-Methoxybenzoic < 4-Methylbenzoic < Benzoic < 4-Nitrobenzoic.

💡 Hint: EWG increase acidity; EDG decrease it.
📌 Q 8.15: Complete reactions (i) C6H5COCH3 →Zn-Hg/HCl (ii) CH3CH2COOH →Br2/red P (iii) C6H5CHO →conc. NaOH

Answer: (i) C6H5CH2CH3 (Clemmensen)
(ii) CH3CHBrCOOH (HVZ)
(iii) C6H5CH2OH + C6H5COONa (Cannizzaro).

💡 Hint: Identify reaction type from reagents.

Frequently Asked Conversions

🔄 Convert ethanal to but-2-enal

Pathway: Aldol condensation: 2 CH3CHO →dil. NaOH CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO →Δ, –H2O CH3CH=CHCHO.

💡 Hint: Use aldol addition followed by dehydration.
🔄 Convert acetone to tert‑butyl alcohol

Pathway: CH3COCH3 + CH3MgBr →dry ether →H3O+ (CH3)3COH.

💡 Hint: Grignard addition to ketone gives 3° alcohol.
🔄 Convert benzoic acid to benzaldehyde

Pathway: C6H5COOH →SOCl2 C6H5COCl →H2/Pd-BaSO4 C6H5CHO (Rosenmund).

💡 Hint: Acid → acid chloride → Rosenmund reduction.
🔄 Convert toluene to benzaldehyde

Pathway: Etard reaction: C6H5CH3 →CrO2Cl2/CS2 →H2O C6H5CHO.

💡 Hint: Chromyl chloride oxidation stops at aldehyde.
🔄 Convert ethanoic acid to methane

Pathway: CH3COOH →NaOH CH3COONa →NaOH/CaO, Δ CH4 (decarboxylation).

💡 Hint: Soda lime decarboxylation.
🔄 Convert propanone to propene

Pathway: CH3COCH3 →NaBH4 CH3CHOHCH3 →conc. H2SO4, Δ CH3CH=CH2 (dehydration).

💡 Hint: Reduce ketone to alcohol, then dehydrate.

Important PYQs (Board Exams)

📝 Why does benzaldehyde not give Fehling's test? (CBSE 2023, 2022)

Answer: Benzaldehyde cannot reduce Fehling's solution because the –CHO group is directly attached to the benzene ring, which stabilises it against oxidation under alkaline conditions. It gives positive Tollens' test.

💡 Hint: Aromatic aldehydes are resistant to Fehling's.
📝 Predict the product: C6H5COCH3 + NaOH/I2 → ? (CBSE 2024)

Answer: Yellow precipitate of iodoform (CHI3) + C6H5COONa. Iodoform test for methyl ketone.

💡 Hint: Methyl ketone gives iodoform reaction.
📝 How will you distinguish between propanal and propanone? (CBSE 2021)

Answer: Tollens' test – propanal gives silver mirror; propanone does not. (Or Fehling's test – propanal gives red ppt.)

💡 Hint: Aldehydes are easily oxidised.
📝 Explain the mechanism of aldol condensation. (CBSE 2019)

Answer: Base abstracts α‑H → enolate ion → attacks another carbonyl carbon → aldol (β‑hydroxy carbonyl) → dehydration → α,β‑unsaturated carbonyl.

💡 Hint: Nucleophilic addition of enolate to carbonyl.
📝 Give a chemical test to distinguish between ethanoic acid and ethanal. (CBSE 2020)

Answer: NaHCO3 test: ethanoic acid gives brisk effervescence of CO2; ethanal does not. (Or Tollens' test for ethanal)

💡 Hint: Carboxylic acid liberates CO2 with bicarbonate.
📝 Arrange in increasing acidity: CH3COOH, ClCH2COOH, Cl2CHCOOH, CCl3COOH. (CBSE 2018)

Answer: CH3COOH < ClCH2COOH < Cl2CHCOOH < CCl3COOH. More EWG → stronger acid.

💡 Hint: Inductive effect of chlorine increases acidity.
