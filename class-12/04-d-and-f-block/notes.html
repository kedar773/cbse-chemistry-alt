⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected Why Cr and Cu are exceptions? [See Solved Q 8.1]
Cr: 3d5 4s1 (not 3d4 4s2) because half-filled d5 confers extra stability through exchange energy and symmetrical distribution.
Cu: 3d10 4s1 (not 3d9 4s2) because fully-filled d10 confers extra stability.

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected Why do transition elements show variable oxidation states? [See Solved Q 8.4]
Due to the availability of (n–1)d and ns orbitals with similar energies. The (n–1)d electrons can also participate in bonding as the energy difference between ns and (n–1)d is small.
⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected Why is Cu2+ more stable than Cu+ in aqueous medium? [See Solved Q 8.5]
Cu2+ has higher hydration enthalpy (enthalpy of hydration) which compensates for the higher IE2. Cu+ disproportionates: 2Cu+ → Cu + Cu2+ (ΔG° negative).

⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected Why are transition metal ions colored? [See Solved Q 8.6]
Due to d-d transitions. In the presence of ligands, the d-orbitals split into two sets (t2g and eg). Electrons absorb visible light to jump from lower to higher d-orbitals. The complementary color is observed.

5. Catalytic Properties

Transition metals and their compounds act as catalysts because:

  • Variable oxidation states allow them to form intermediates
  • Large surface area for adsorption (in heterogeneous catalysis)

Examples: V2O5 (Contact process for H2SO4), Fe (Haber process for NH3), Ni (hydrogenation), Pd/Pt (hydrogenation, catalytic converters). ⚡ CBSE PYQ [See Solved Q 8.7]

6. Interstitial Compounds

Formed when small atoms (H, B, C, N) occupy interstitial voids in the metal lattice. Properties: hard, high melting point, metallic conductivity retained.

7. Alloys

Transition metals form alloys due to similar atomic radii. Examples: Stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni), Brass (Cu + Zn), Bronze (Cu + Sn).

8. Oxoanions of d-Block Elements

Potassium Dichromate (K2Cr2O7)

Preparation: 4FeCr2O4 + 8Na2CO3 + 7O2 → 2Fe2O3 + 8Na2CrO4 + 8CO2 → Na2Cr2O7 → K2Cr2O7 (orange crystals).

Oxidizing reactions (Acidic medium):
Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O (E° = +1.33 V)

Structure: Two CrO4 tetrahedra sharing an oxygen atom. Cr—O—Cr bridge.

Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4)

Preparation: 2MnO2 + 4KOH + O2 → 2K2MnO4 + 2H2O; then 3K2MnO4 + 4CO2 → 2KMnO4 + MnO2 + 4K2CO3 (or electrolytic oxidation).

Oxidizing reactions:
Acidic: MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O (E° = +1.51 V)
Neutral: MnO4− + 2H2O + 3e− → MnO2 + 4OH− (E° = +0.59 V)
Alkaline: MnO4− + e− → MnO42− (E° = +0.56 V)

3. Oxidation States

Lanthanoids: Predominantly +3. Some show +2 (Eu, Yb due to f7, f14 stability) and +4 (Ce, Tb). ⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

Actinoids: Show broader range (+3 to +7) because 5f, 6d, and 7s orbitals have closer energies.

4. Lanthanoid Contraction

Definition: The steady decrease in atomic/ionic radii of lanthanoids with increasing atomic number (from Ce to Lu). ⚡ CBSE PYQ - Expected

Cause: Poor shielding of 4f electrons. As nuclear charge increases by +1 across the series, the 4f electrons are not effective at shielding each other, so the effective nuclear charge (Zeff) increases.

Consequences:

  • Similar radii of 4d and 5d elements (e.g., Zr & Hf, Nb & Ta, Mo & W) — Zr-Hf pair is nearly identical in properties
  • Difficulty in separating lanthanoids
  • Basic strength of hydroxides decreases across the series

[See Solved Q 8.9]

NCERT Solutions — d- & f-Block Elements (Chapter 8)

Board Exam Focus Subset marked with ⚡ PYQ.

Q 8.1 ⚡ PYQ Write the electronic configurations of Cr and Cu. Why are they exceptional?

Cr (Z = 24): Expected [Ar] 3d4 4s2 but actual is [Ar] 3d5 4s1.
Cu (Z = 29): Expected [Ar] 3d9 4s2 but actual is [Ar] 3d10 4s1.

Reason: Half-filled (d5) and fully-filled (d10) configurations provide extra stability due to:

  • Greater exchange energy (electrons with parallel spins exchange energy)
  • Symmetric distribution of electron density
  • Lower energy due to reduced electron-electron repulsion
Q 8.2 ⚡ PYQ Explain the trend in enthalpy of atomization of 3d series.

Trend: Increases from Sc (326) to V (515), then decreases with irregular pattern to Zn (130 kJ/mol).

Reason: Atomization enthalpy depends on metallic bond strength, which depends on the number of unpaired d-electrons available for bonding. Sc (d1): weak; V (d3): maximum due to 3 unpaired e−; Cr (d5): half-filled reduces bonding effectiveness; Mn (d5): weak; Zn (d10): no unpaired electrons → weakest metallic bonding.

Values: Sc 326, Ti 473, V 515, Cr 397, Mn 281, Fe 416, Co 425, Ni 430, Cu 338, Zn 130 (kJ/mol).

Q 8.3 ⚡ PYQ Explain the anomalous electrode potential values of Mn and Cu.

Mn: E°(Mn2+/Mn) = −1.18 V (more negative than expected). The Mn2+ (d5) has extra stability, making it easier to form, so the reduction potential is more negative.

Cu: E°(Cu2+/Cu) = +0.34 V (positive, unlike other 3d metals). This is due to: (a) high sublimation enthalpy of Cu, (b) not-so-high hydration enthalpy of Cu2+, and (c) high IE2 of Cu.

Zn: E°(Zn2+/Zn) = −0.76 V (less negative than expected for d10), due to the stable d10 configuration requiring more energy to ionize.

Q 8.4 ⚡ PYQ Why do transition elements show variable oxidation states?

Transition elements show variable oxidation states because the (n−1)d and ns orbitals have very close energies. Both ns and (n−1)d electrons can participate in bonding. As we move across a series, the number of d-electrons changes, giving rise to different possible oxidation states.

Examples: Mn shows +2, +3, +4, +6, +7; V shows +2, +3, +4, +5; Fe shows +2, +3.

Factors that stabilize a particular oxidation state: (a) d5 and d10 configurations, (b) nature of anion/ligand, (c) hydration/solvation energy, (d) crystal field stabilization energy (CFSE).

Q 8.5 ⚡ PYQ Why is Cu2+ more stable than Cu+ in aqueous solution?

Cu+ (3d10) in aqueous solution disproportionates:
2Cu+(aq) → Cu(s) + Cu2+(aq)

This is because Cu2+ (3d9) has much higher hydration enthalpy (ΔhydH°) which compensates for the higher second ionization enthalpy (IE2) of Cu. The overall ΔG° for the disproportionation is negative.

In the solid state (e.g., Cu2O), Cu+ is stable because the lattice energy compensates.

Q 8.6 ⚡ PYQ Why are transition metal compounds generally colored?

Transition metal ions absorb visible light due to d-d transitions. In a free transition metal ion, all d-orbitals have the same energy. However, in the presence of ligands (crystal field splitting), the d-orbitals split into two sets: t2g (lower energy) and eg (higher energy).

Electrons in the lower d-orbitals absorb specific wavelengths of visible light to jump to higher d-orbitals. The color observed is the complementary color of the absorbed light.

Note: Sc3+ (3d0) and Zn2+ (3d10) are colorless because no d-d transition is possible (no unpaired electrons to promote).

Q 8.7 ⚡ PYQ Explain catalytic properties of transition metals.

Transition metals and their compounds act as catalysts because:

  1. Variable oxidation states: They can form unstable intermediate compounds by changing their oxidation state, providing a new pathway with lower activation energy.
  2. Surface adsorption: Transition metals provide a large surface area for reactant adsorption, weakening bonds in reactants.

Examples:

  • V2O5 in Contact process: 2SO2 + O2 → 2SO3 (V5+ ↔ V4+)
  • Fe (finely divided) in Haber process: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3
  • Ni in hydrogenation of oils
  • Pt/Pd in catalytic converters for automobile exhaust
Q 8.8 ⚡ PYQ Describe the oxidizing action of KMnO4 and K2Cr2O7.

K2Cr2O7 in acidic medium:
Cr2O72− + 14H+ + 6e− → 2Cr3+ + 7H2O (E° = +1.33 V)
Color change: Orange → Green (Cr3+).

Examples:
Cr2O72− + 6Fe2+ + 14H+ → 2Cr3+ + 6Fe3+ + 7H2O
Cr2O72− + 6I− + 14H+ → 2Cr3+ + 3I2 + 7H2O

KMnO4 in different media:

  • Acidic: MnO4− + 8H+ + 5e− → Mn2+ + 4H2O (E° = +1.51 V)
    Purple → Colorless/faint pink
  • Neutral: MnO4− + 2H2O + 3e− → MnO2 + 4OH− (E° = +0.59 V)
    Purple → Brown (MnO2 precipitate)
  • Alkaline: MnO4− + e− → MnO42− (E° = +0.56 V)
    Purple → Green (manganate ion)

Examples in acidic medium:
2MnO4− + 5C2O42− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O
2MnO4− + 10I− + 16H+ → 2Mn2+ + 5I2 + 8H2O

Q 8.9 ⚡ PYQ What is lanthanoid contraction? What are its consequences?

Lanthanoid contraction: The steady decrease in atomic/ionic radii of lanthanoids (Ce to Lu) with increasing atomic number.

Cause: Poor shielding of 4f electrons. As nuclear charge increases, the effective nuclear charge (Zeff) felt by outer electrons increases, pulling them inwards.

Consequences:

  1. Similar radii of 4d and 5d elements: Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm) have nearly identical radii. Same for Nb-Ta, Mo-W. This makes separation of these pairs very difficult.
  2. Difficulty in lanthanoid separation: All lanthanoids have similar chemical properties due to similar radii.
  3. Decreasing basic strength: The basic strength of lanthanoid hydroxides [Ln(OH)3] decreases from Ce(OH)3 to Lu(OH)3.
  4. Ionic radii trend: Ln3+ radii decrease from 103 pm (Ce3+) to 86 pm (Lu3+).

Additional NCERT Exercise Questions

Q 8.10 Which of the 3d series elements shows the maximum number of oxidation states and why?

Manganese (Mn) shows the maximum number of oxidation states: +2, +3, +4, +6, +7. This is because Mn has the electronic configuration [Ar] 3d5 4s2, and the 5 d-electrons along with the 2 s-electrons can be progressively removed (total 7 electrons) due to close energy of 3d and 4s orbitals.

Q 8.11 Assign reasons: (a) E°(M2+/M) for copper is positive. (b) Cr2+ is reducing while Mn3+ is oxidizing.

(a) Cu has high sublimation enthalpy, high IE2, and relatively low hydration enthalpy, making the overall process less favorable, giving a positive E°.

(b) Cr2+ (3d4) easily loses one electron to form stable Cr3+ (3d3 half-filled t2g) → reducing. Mn3+ (3d4) easily gains an electron to form stable Mn2+ (3d5 half-filled d-shell) → oxidizing.

Q 8.12 What are interstitial compounds? Give their characteristics.

Interstitial compounds are formed when small atoms (H, B, C, N) occupy interstitial voids in the transition metal lattice.

Characteristics: (a) Very hard and brittle, (b) High melting points, (c) Retain metallic conductivity, (d) Chemically inert.

Q 8.13 Why do transition metals form alloys? Give examples.

Transition metals form alloys because their atomic radii are very similar, allowing one metal to substitute for another in the crystal lattice.

Examples: Stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni), Brass (Cu + Zn), Bronze (Cu + Sn), Nichrome (Ni + Cr).

Q 8.14 Describe the preparation of K2Cr2O7 from chromite ore.

Steps:

  1. Fusion of chromite (FeCr2O4) with Na2CO3 in air:
    4FeCr2O4 + 8Na2CO3 + 7O2 → 2Fe2O3 + 8Na2CrO4 + 8CO2
  2. Leaching with water and filtration
  3. Acidification of Na2CrO4 with H2SO4:
    2Na2CrO4 + H2SO4 → Na2Cr2O7 + Na2SO4 + H2O
  4. Conversion to potassium salt:
    Na2Cr2O7 + 2KCl → K2Cr2O7 + 2NaCl

Orange crystals of K2Cr2O7 are obtained.

Q 8.15 Describe the preparation of KMnO4 from pyrolusite ore.

Steps:

  1. Fusion of MnO2 (pyrolusite) with KOH and oxidant (KNO3 or air):
    2MnO2 + 4KOH + O2 → 2K2MnO4 + 2H2O
  2. Oxidation of K2MnO4 to KMnO4:
    Method 1 (chemical): 3K2MnO4 + 4CO2 → 2KMnO4 + MnO2 + 4K2CO3
    Method 2 (electrolytic): Anodic oxidation of K2MnO4 at a Ni electrode.

Deep purple/black crystals of KMnO4 are obtained.

Q 8.16 Discuss the structure of chromate (CrO42−) and dichromate (Cr2O72−) ions.

Chromate (CrO42−): Tetrahedral structure. Cr is sp3 hybridized. Four O atoms at corners of tetrahedron. Cr–O bond length ~165 pm. Yellow color.

Dichromate (Cr2O72−): Two CrO4 tetrahedra sharing one oxygen at the common vertex. Cr–O–Cr bond angle ~126°. Terminal Cr–O bonds are shorter than the bridging Cr–O–Cr bonds. Orange color.

Interconversion in solution:
2CrO42− + 2H+ ↔ Cr2O72− + H2O
Yellow (chromate) in basic/neutral, Orange (dichromate) in acidic.

Q 8.17 What are the oxidation states of lanthanoids and actinoids? How do they differ?

Lanthanoids: Most common is +3. Ce also shows +4 (f0 stable), Eu shows +2 (f7 stable), Tb shows +4, Yb shows +2 (f14 stable). The +3 state is the most stable for all lanthanoids.

Actinoids: Show a wider range of oxidation states: +3, +4, +5, +6, +7 (e.g., U shows +3, +4, +5, +6; Np shows +3 to +7). This is because 5f, 6d, and 7s orbitals have very close energies.

Difference: Actinoids exhibit higher oxidation states (+5 to +7) more commonly than lanthanoids due to poorer shielding of 5f electrons making them more available for bonding.

Q 8.19 Explain why: (a) Transition metals act as catalysts. (b) Zn, Cd, and Hg are not transition metals.

(a) Already covered in Q 8.7. Variable oxidation states and ability to form intermediates.

(b) Zn (d10), Cd (d10), and Hg (d10) have completely filled d-orbitals in their ground state and in their common oxidation state (+2). They do not have partially filled d-orbitals in any of their compounds, which is a requirement for being a transition element (as per IUPAC: element whose atom has partially filled d-orbitals in ground state or in any of its oxidation states).

Q 8.20 Calculate the magnetic moment of Fe3+ and Cr3+ ions.

Fe3+: Electronic config = [Ar] 3d5. Number of unpaired electrons (n) = 5.
μ = √n(n+2) = √5(7) = √35 = 5.92 BM

Cr3+: Electronic config = [Ar] 3d3. Number of unpaired electrons (n) = 3.
μ = √n(n+2) = √3(5) = √15 = 3.87 BM
