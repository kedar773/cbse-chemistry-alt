Concentration Terms

1. Molarity (M)

M = nsolute / Vsolution (L)

Temperature-dependent. CBSE PYQ

2. Molality (m)

m = nsolute / Wsolvent (kg)

Temperature-independent. Preferred in colligative calculations.

3. Mole Fraction (x)

xA = nA / (nA + nB)  ;  xA + xB = 1
[See Q2.5]

Henry's Law

Solubility of a gas is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid.

p = KH · x
  • Higher KH → lower solubility. CBSE PYQ
  • KH increases with temperature → solubility decreases (warm soda goes flat).
  • Applications: Soft drinks (sealed under high CO2), Scuba diving (N2 solubility in blood → "bends").
[See Q2.6]

Raoult's Law

Volatile Solute + Volatile Solvent

ptotal = pA°·xA + pB°·xB

Non-Volatile Solute

Relative lowering = (pA° − ps) / pA° = xB
CBSE PYQ [See Q2.8]

Ideal Solutions

  • ΔHmix = 0, ΔVmix = 0
  • A–A / B–B interactions ≈ A–B interactions
  • Examples: n-Hexane + n-Heptane, Benzene + Toluene

Elevation of Boiling Point

Adding non-volatile solute lowers VP → higher T needed to boil.

ΔTb = Tb − Tb° = i · Kb · m
CBSE PYQ [See Q2.17]

Depression of Freezing Point

ΔTf = Tf° − Tf = i · Kf · m
MB = i · Kf · wB · 1000 / (ΔTf · wA)
Most Tested [See Q2.11, Q2.21]

Osmotic Pressure (Π)

Π = i · C · R · T = i · (wB/MB) · (RT/V)
  • Measured at room temp (suitable for biomolecules)
  • Dilute solutions give measurable Π
  • Reverse Osmosis: Apply P > Π to purify water.
CBSE PYQ [See Q2.20, Q2.22]

Van 't Hoff Factor (i)

i = Observed colligative property / Normal colligative property

Dissociation (e.g., NaCl, KCl)

i = 1 + (n − 1) · α

Association (e.g., Acetic acid in benzene)

i = 1 − (1 − 1/n) · α

NCERT Textbook Solutions

Q2.1 Calculate the mass percentage of benzene (C6H6) and carbon tetrachloride (CCl4) if 22 g of benzene is dissolved in 122 g of CCl4.
Total mass of solution = 22 + 122 = 144 g
Mass % of benzene = (22 / 144) × 100 = 15.28%
Mass % of CCl4 = (122 / 144) × 100 = 84.72%
Q2.5 Calculate the mole fraction of benzene in a solution containing 30% by mass in CCl4. Expected
Let total mass = 100 g. Mass of C6H6 = 30 g, Mass of CCl4 = 70 g
n(C6H6) = 30 / 78 = 0.385 mol; n(CCl4) = 70 / 154 = 0.455 mol
x(C6H6) = 0.385 / (0.385 + 0.455) = 0.458
Q2.6 Henry's law constant for CO2 in water is 1.67 × 108 Pa at 298 K. Calculate the solubility when its pressure is 0.987 bar. CBSE PYQ
p = 0.987 bar = 98700 Pa. By Henry's law: x = p / KH
x = 98700 / (1.67 × 108) = 5.91 × 10-4
Q2.8 The vapor pressure of pure benzene at a certain temperature is 0.850 bar. A non-volatile solid (0.5 g) is added to 39.0 g of benzene (M = 78). The vapor pressure of the solution is 0.845 bar. What is the molar mass of the solid? CBSE PYQ
Relative lowering: (0.850 - 0.845) / 0.850 = 0.00588 = xB
nA = 39.0 / 78 = 0.5 mol
xB ≈ nB/nA → 0.00588 = (0.5 / MB) / 0.5
MB = 1 / 0.00588 = 170 g mol-1
Q2.11 0.5 g of KCl dissolved in 100 g of water freezes at -0.24°C. Calculate the degree of dissociation of KCl. (Kf = 1.86) Board Favourite
Molality (observed) = ΔTf / Kf = 0.24 / 1.86 = 0.129 m
Molality (calculated) = (0.5/74.5) × (1000/100) = 0.0671 m
i = 0.129 / 0.0671 = 1.922
For KCl: n = 2 → i = 1 + α → 1.922 = 1 + α
α = 0.922 (92.2%)
Q2.17 18 g of glucose is dissolved in 1 kg of water. At what temperature will water boil at 1.013 bar? (Kb = 0.52) Expected
m = (18/180) × (1000/1000) = 0.1 mol kg-1
ΔTb = 0.52 × 0.1 = 0.052°C
Boiling point = 100 + 0.052 = 100.052°C
Q2.20 A 5% (w/V) solution of cane sugar (M=342) is isotonic with 0.877% solution of X. Find molar mass of X. CBSE PYQ
Csugar = (5/342) × (1000/100) = 0.146 M
CX = 8.77 / MX. Since isotonic: Csugar = CX
MX = 8.77 / 0.146 = 60.07 g mol-1 (Urea)
