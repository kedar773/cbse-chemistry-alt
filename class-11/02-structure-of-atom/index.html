Rutherford's Nuclear Model Positively charged dense nucleus at center; electrons orbiting at distance; explains α-particle scattering results Gold foil experiment: some α-particles scatter back (10⁻⁵ probability); implies concentrated positive charge Rejected Thomson's "plum pudding" model (uniform positive sphere with electrons); established nuclear model foundation α-particle scattering formula gives nuclear charge; forward scattering rules out distributed charge 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐
Bohr's Atomic Model (Hydrogen) Electron orbits nucleus at discrete radii (energy levels); angular momentum quantized: L = nℏ where ℏ = h/(2π); electrons jump between orbits by absorbing/emitting photons Orbital radius: $r_n = \frac{n^2 \hbar^2}{Ze^2 m_e}$ (Bohr formula, SI units complex); Energy: $E_n = -\frac{Z^2 \times 13.6 \text{ eV}}{n^2}$ Explains hydrogen spectrum (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen series); discrete lines from transitions between quantized levels Successful for hydrogen; fails for multi-electron atoms (shielding effects); foundation for quantum mechanics 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Quantum Mechanical Model (Wave Nature) Electron described by wave function Ψ(x,y,z); Ψ ² probability density (where electron likely found); orbital = region with 90% probability of finding electron Schrödinger equation: $\hat{H}\Psi = E\Psi$ (time-independent); solutions give orbitals & allowed energies Replaces orbits (definite path) with orbitals (probability region); explains chemical bonding, molecular structure Successful for all atoms; complex calculations for multi-electron systems (approximations used)
Photoelectric Effect & Photon Energy Electrons ejected from metal surface when light shines; kinetic energy KE_max = hν − Φ (Φ = work function) Energy of photon: E = hν = hc/λ; threshold frequency: ν₀ = Φ/h; stopping potential: eV_s = hν − Φ Demonstrates wave-particle duality (light behaves as particles with E = hν); explains why frequency matters (not intensity) Einstein equation linear in frequency; explains ultraviolet catastrophe solution 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐

Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle Cannot simultaneously determine position and momentum with arbitrary precision: Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2 Uncertainty product: Δx·Δp ≥ h/(4π); also Δt·ΔE ≥ ℏ/2; smaller Δx → larger Δp and vice versa Explains why orbits undefined (can't know both position and velocity precisely); supports orbital concept (probability region) Fundamental limit on classical description of particles; sets minimum energy (~ℏω/2, zero-point energy) 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐⭐

Effective Nuclear Charge (Z_eff) Charge "felt" by electron after accounting for shielding by inner electrons Z_eff = Z − S where Z = atomic number, S = shielding constant (depends on electron configuration) Each electron experiences not full +Z, but reduced charge (inner electrons reduce outer electron attraction) Use Slater's rules: assign shielding value to each electron pair; sum to get S; Z_eff = Z − S Fluorine (Z=9): 1s² 2s² 2p⁵ (valence e⁻ in 2p sees Z_eff ≈ 5 due to shielding by 1s² & 2s²); Chlorine (Z=17) valence: Z_eff ≈ 7 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐

Photon Energy E = hν = hc/λ where h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s Energy of single photon proportional to frequency (not intensity); higher frequency = higher energy per photon E = hν (use ν in Hz); or E = hc/λ (use λ in meters); 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J Red light (λ = 700 nm): E = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸) / (700 × 10⁻⁹) ≈ 2.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 1.77 eV 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐
Photoelectric Effect (Einstein) KE_max = hν − Φ where Φ = work function (minimum energy to remove electron); threshold: ν₀ = Φ/h Electrons ejected only if photon energy > work function; KE_max independent of light intensity (depends on frequency) Measure stopping potential V_s: eV_s = KE_max = hν − Φ; plot V_s vs. ν → slope = h/e, intercept = −Φ/e Potassium (Φ = 2.3 eV): threshold ν₀ = 2.3 eV / (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) = 5.57 × 10¹⁴ Hz; violet light (ν > ν₀) ejects electrons; red (ν < ν₀) does not 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Work Function & Stopping Potential Φ = hν − KE_max; stopping potential V_s: eV_s = KE_max = hν − Φ Work function characteristic of metal; stopping potential stops even fastest electrons (opposite polarity applied) Calculate Φ from threshold frequency: Φ = hν₀; or from photoelectric data: use linear plot to find slope & intercept Metal with Φ = 3.5 eV; photon ν = 2 × 10¹⁵ Hz: KE_max = hν − Φ = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 2 × 10¹⁵) − 3.5 eV = 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁸ J − 5.61 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 7.7 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 4.8 eV 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
