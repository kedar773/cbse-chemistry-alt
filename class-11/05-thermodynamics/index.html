SECTION 1: ENTHALPY (ΔH) — HEAT AT CONSTANT PRESSURE

PART A: Enthalpy Definition & Relationship to Internal Energy

Concept Definition Formula Derivation Physical Meaning Example PYQ Tags JEE Level
Enthalpy (H) State function defined as H = U + PV; measures heat exchanged at constant pressure ΔH = ΔU + Δ(PV) = ΔU + P·ΔV (at constant P) From First Law: q_p = ΔU + P·ΔV; define H so q_p = ΔH Constant-pressure heat (lab conditions); more practical than ΔU because most reactions occur at constant P Combustion of 1 mol CH₄: ΔH_comb ≈ −890 kJ/mol (heat released at 1 atm, 298 K) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐
Relationship: ΔH vs. ΔU ΔH = ΔU + Δ(PV); at constant P: ΔH = ΔU + P·ΔV For ideal gas: P·ΔV = Δ(nRT) = ΔnRT (at constant T); ΔH = ΔU + ΔnRT ΔH includes work done against atmospheric pressure (P·ΔV term) in addition to internal energy change Small for condensed phases (liquids, solids) where ΔV << volume; significant for gases where Δn·RT substantial H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O(l): ΔH ≈ −286 kJ/mol; ΔU ≈ −242 kJ/mol; difference = 1 mol × 8.314 × 298 / 1000 ≈ 2.5 kJ (small for liquid product) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 ⭐⭐
Exothermic vs. Endothermic Exothermic: ΔH < 0 (heat released to surroundings); Endothermic: ΔH > 0 (heat absorbed from surroundings) ΔH negative = spontaneous at low T (if ΔS not too negative); ΔH positive = requires heat input Sign indicates energy flow direction; magnitude indicates heat quantity Combustion: ΔH << 0 (strong bond breaking/formation); melting: ΔH > 0 (endothermic, absorbs heat) CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l), ΔH ≈ −890 kJ/mol (exothermic); ice melting: ΔH = +6.01 kJ/mol (endothermic) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐
Heat of Reaction (ΔH_rxn) Enthalpy change for reaction as written; depends on stoichiometric coefficients and physical states ΔH_rxn = Σ(ΔH_f,products) − Σ(ΔH_f,reactants); also ΔH_rxn = Σ(bond energies broken) − Σ(bond energies formed) For molar quantity: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O has ΔH_rxn = 2 × ΔH_f(H₂O); ½H₂ + ¼O₂ → ½H₂O has ΔH_rxn = ½ × ΔH_f(H₂O) Extensive property; doubles if coefficients doubled; sign reverses if reaction reversed N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ/mol (exo); reverse: ΔH = +92 kJ/mol (endo) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐
Heat of Formation (ΔH_f°) Enthalpy change when 1 mole of compound formed from its elements in standard states (25°C, 1 atm) Elements in standard state: ΔH_f° = 0; for compound: ΔH_f° = heat released/absorbed to form 1 mol Use to calculate ΔH_rxn via: ΔH_rxn = Σ(n × ΔH_f°,products) − Σ(n × ΔH_f°,reactants) Reference values tabulated; ΔH_f° for elements = 0 by definition H₂O(l): ΔH_f° = −285.8 kJ/mol; CO₂(g): ΔH_f° = −393.5 kJ/mol; use to compute combustion reactions 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Heat of Combustion (ΔH_comb) Enthalpy change when 1 mole of substance completely oxidized (burned) to stable oxides (CO₂, H₂O, etc.) For hydrocarbon C_xH_y + excess O₂ → x CO₂ + (y/2) H₂O; ΔH_comb = Σ ΔH_f°(products) − ΔH_f°(reactant) Always negative (exothermic); large negative values reflect stability of products (CO₂, H₂O) Bond breaking (endothermic, ΔH > 0) and bond formation (exothermic, ΔH < 0) net to ΔH_comb < 0 CH₄(g): ΔH_comb ≈ −890 kJ/mol; C(graphite): ΔH_comb ≈ −394 kJ/mol; glucose C₆H₁₂O₆: ΔH_comb ≈ −2803 kJ/mol 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐

SECTION 2: HESS'S LAW & THERMOCHEMICAL CALCULATIONS

PART A: Hess's Law & Manipulation of Thermochemical Equations

Principle Statement Mathematical Form Application Example Verification PYQ Tags JEE Level
Hess's Law (State Function Consequence) ΔH_reaction depends only on initial and final states, not on pathway; sum of ΔH values for series of reactions = ΔH for overall reaction If Reaction 1 (A → B): ΔH₁ and Reaction 2 (B → C): ΔH₂, then A → C: ΔH_net = ΔH₁ + ΔH₂ Foundation for calculating ΔH_rxn from tabulated ΔH_f° values; allows derivation of unknown ΔH from known reactions (1) C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g): ΔH = −393.5 kJ; (2) 2CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l) → CH₃COOH(l) + 2O₂(g): ΔH = +1020 kJ [reverse of combustion]; (3) 2C(s) + 2H₂O(l) → CH₃COOH(l) + O₂(g): ΔH = 2 × (−393.5) + 1020 = 233 kJ [sum of (1)×2 + (2)] Hess's Law valid because H is state function; ∮ dH = 0 (cyclic path); alternative pathways give same ΔH_net 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Reversing a Reaction If A + B → C has ΔH = −100 kJ, then C → A + B has ΔH = +100 kJ Reverse reaction: ΔH_reverse = −ΔH_forward Heat absorbed becomes heat released and vice versa; sign flips N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g): ΔH = −92 kJ (exo); Reverse: 2NH₃(g) → N₂(g) + 3H₂(g): ΔH = +92 kJ (endo) Endothermic processes require energy input; exothermic release energy; reversing flips energy requirement 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 ⭐
Scaling (Multiplying) Reactions If a A + b B → c C has ΔH, then scaling by factor n: n(a A + b B → c C) has ΔH_n = n × ΔH Doubling coefficients doubles ΔH; halving coefficients halves ΔH; general: ΔH_scaled = n × ΔH_original ΔH is extensive (depends on amount); combustion of 1 mol vs. 2 mol differs by factor of 2 CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l): ΔH = −890 kJ; ½CH₄(g) + O₂(g) → ½CO₂(g) + H₂O(l): ΔH = −445 kJ (half) Accounting for stoichiometric coefficients; if reaction scaled by fraction, ΔH scales by same fraction 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐
Combining Multiple Reactions Series of reactions: (1) A → B (ΔH₁), (2) B → C (ΔH₂), (3) C → D (ΔH₃); Overall: A → D (ΔH_net = ΔH₁ + ΔH₂ + ΔH₃) Add ΔH values algebraically; reverse + scale as needed Derive ΔH for target reaction from set of given reactions; systematic cancellation of intermediates Target: C + O₂ → CO₂. Given: (1) 2C + 2O₂ → 2CO₂ (ΔH = −787 kJ); (2) C + ½O₂ → CO (ΔH = −110.5 kJ); Solution: Use (1)×½ = C + O₂ → CO₂ (ΔH = −393.5 kJ); or use (1) − (2) manipulations Check intermediate cancellation; ensure target reaction matches combination of given reactions exactly 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐⭐

Method Formula Approach Advantage Limitation Example PYQ Tags JEE Level
Using ΔH_f° Values (Tabulated) $\Delta H_{rxn}° = \sum \Delta H_{f}°(products) - \sum \Delta H_{f}°(reactants)$ Look up ΔH_f° for each compound from tables; multiply by stoichiometric coefficients; sum products, subtract reactants Simple, fast, no need to construct reaction combinations; ΔH_f° widely tabulated for common compounds Requires accurate ΔH_f° values; not practical if values unavailable; assumes standard states N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g): ΔH_f°(N₂) = 0, ΔH_f°(H₂) = 0, ΔH_f°(NH₃) = −45.9 kJ/mol; ΔH_rxn = 2(−45.9) − [0 + 3(0)] = −91.8 kJ/mol ✓ 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Using Bond Dissociation Energies $\Delta H_{rxn} = \sum D_e(\text{bonds broken}) - \sum D_e(\text{bonds formed})$ Sum all bond-breaking energies (endothermic, +); subtract sum of bond-forming energies (exothermic, −); net = ΔH_rxn Applicable to any reaction; bond energies relatively constant across molecules; allows estimation when ΔH_f° unavailable Approximation (average bond energies vary with bonding context); less accurate than ΔH_f°; requires detailed bond identification CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O: Bonds broken: 4(C−H) + 2(O=O) = 4(414) + 2(498) = 2652 kJ; Bonds formed: 2(C=O) + 4(O−H) = 2(799) + 4(463) = 3454 kJ; ΔH = 2652 − 3454 = −802 kJ/mol (literature ≈ −890 kJ; discrepancy due to bond energy approximations) 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Hess's Law Problem (Multi-Step Derivation) Design combination of given reactions to target reaction; reverse, scale, and add; verify intermediate cancellation (1) Identify target reaction; (2) List given reactions; (3) Manipulate (reverse/scale) to align with target; (4) Add ΔH values; (5) Check for intermediate cancellation Develops problem-solving skills; tests understanding of state function and algebraic manipulation Complex for multi-step combinations; easy to make algebraic errors; requires careful bookkeeping Example: Target: 2C + O₂ → 2CO. Given: (1) C + O₂ → CO₂ (ΔH = −393.5 kJ); (2) 2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂ (ΔH = −566 kJ). Solution: (1) − ½(2) = C + O₂ − CO − ½O₂ → CO₂ − CO₂; simplify: C + ½O₂ → CO (ΔH = −393.5 − ½ × (−566) = −393.5 + 283 = −110.5 kJ); scale ×2: 2C + O₂ → 2CO (ΔH = −221 kJ) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐⭐

SECTION 3: ENTROPY (ΔS) — SPONTANEITY & DISORDER

PART A: Entropy Definition & Trends

Concept Definition Formula/Expression Physical Meaning Trend Example PYQ Tags JEE Level
Entropy (S) State function measuring disorder/randomness at molecular level; microscopic interpretation: S = k_B ln Ω where Ω = number of microstates $\Delta S = \int \frac{dq_{rev}}{T}$ (definitional); for reversible isothermal: ΔS = q_rev/T Disorder increases; perfect crystal at 0 K has S = 0 (Third Law); entropy of universe always increases (Second Law) S_solid < S_liquid < S_gas (at same T); S increases with molecular complexity; S increases with T Ice: S ≈ 39.3 J/mol·K; Water: S ≈ 69.9 J/mol·K; Steam: S ≈ 188.7 J/mol·K (at 298 K, 1 atm) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Third Law of Thermodynamics Perfect crystal at absolute zero (0 K) has entropy S = 0; entropy of substance at T > 0 K is positive and finite $S(T) = \int_0^T \frac{C_p}{T} dT$ (entropy from 0 K to T via heat capacity) Allows absolute entropy values (unlike enthalpy, which is relative); reference point for entropy calculations Absolute entropy always ≥ 0; used to calculate ΔS_rxn from tabulated S° values S°(H₂, 298 K) = 130.7 J/mol·K; S°(O₂, 298 K) = 205.1 J/mol·K (reference values) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 ⭐⭐
Standard Molar Entropy (S°) Entropy of 1 mole of substance in standard state (25°C, 1 atm, most stable form) Tabulated values; used to calculate ΔS_rxn = Σ(S°_products) − Σ(S°_reactants) Reference values for all substances; analogous to ΔH_f° but for entropy S° increases with molecular mass (larger molecules, more disorder); S° increases with molecular complexity S°(O₂, g) = 205.1 J/mol·K > S°(O, g) = 160.9 J/mol·K (diatomic > monatomic) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Entropy Change in Reactions (ΔS_rxn) $\Delta S_{rxn}° = \sum n_i S°(products) - \sum n_i S°(reactants)$ ΔS > 0: disorder increases (favors spontaneity); ΔS < 0: disorder decreases (opposes spontaneity) Predict direction based on phase changes and molecular complexity; combine with ΔH to assess overall spontaneity via ΔG Phase change: melting/evaporation (ΔS > 0, disorder ↑); gas formation (ΔS > 0); ion dissolution (often ΔS > 0, solvation disorder) N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g): ΔS° = 2(193) − [191.6 + 3(130.7)] = 386 − 583 = −197 J/mol·K (negative, moles decrease, disorder decreases) 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐
Factors Affecting Entropy (1) Phase: S_gas >> S_liquid > S_solid; (2) Molecular mass: larger → higher S; (3) Molecular complexity: more atoms → higher S; (4) Temperature: higher T → higher S S increases with: temperature, number of molecules, molecular complexity, disorder; S decreases with: lower temperature, phase changes to solid Explains why combustion products (CO₂, H₂O gases) have higher S; why dissolution often ΔS > 0 Gas expansion: ΔS > 0 (disorder ↑); gas compression: ΔS < 0 (disorder ↓); dissolution of ionic solid: ΔS usually > 0 (hydrated ions more disordered) Perfect crystal freezing to lower T: ΔS < 0 (order increases); ice melting: ΔS > 0 (disorder increases) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 ⭐⭐

Gibbs Free Energy (G) G = H − TS; combines enthalpy and entropy effects; state function measuring maximum useful work ΔG = ΔH − TΔS (at constant T, P); $\Delta G = -RT \ln K$ (equilibrium relation) ΔG < 0: spontaneous (forward); ΔG > 0: non-spontaneous (backward); ΔG = 0: equilibrium ΔG ≠ total energy change (ΔU); ΔG = available work for useful purposes (non-PV work) Ice at 25°C and 1 atm: ΔG > 0 (melting non-spontaneous reverse); water at 0°C: ΔG ≈ 0 (equilibrium); water at −10°C: ΔG < 0 (freezing spontaneous) 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐

ΔG° vs. ΔG ΔG° = standard free energy change (standard states: 25°C, 1 atm, 1 M); ΔG = actual free energy change under non-standard conditions $\Delta G = \Delta G° + RT \ln Q$ where Q = reaction quotient; ΔG = −RT ln K at equilibrium (Q = K) ΔG° predicts spontaneity at standard conditions; ΔG predicts at any conditions; at equilibrium, ΔG = 0 ΔG° < 0 → favored at standard conditions; ΔG° > 0 → not favored; but non-standard conditions via ΔG may reverse spontaneity Haber process: ΔG° at 500 K > 0 (not favored by ΔG°), but high pressure (non-standard) shifts equilibrium via Q term, making reaction practical 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐⭐

Temperature Dependence of C_p C_p typically increases with T; for ideal gas C_p constant, but real molecules: C_p(T) via activation of vibrational modes $C_p = a + bT + cT^2$ (empirical fit); quantum levels → higher T allows access to rotational/vibrational states At low T: only translational modes active; at high T: rotational and vibrational contributions increase C_p (more energy storage capacity) Dulong-Petit Law: C_v ≈ 3R per mole for solids (3 quadratic terms per atom: 3 kinetic + 3 potential); breaks down at low T when vibrations frozen out Heat capacity of metals: near 300 K ≈ 3R; at 10 K >> 3R; at T → 0, C_p ∝ T (Debye model) 2024, 2025, 2026 ⭐⭐⭐
