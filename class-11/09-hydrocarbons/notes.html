🧪 Alkanes (CnH2n+2)

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with general formula CnH2n+2. Also called paraffins (Latin: parum affinis). Carbon atoms are sp3 hybridized with tetrahedral geometry (~109.5°).

📝 Methods of Preparation

1. From Unsaturated Hydrocarbons (Sabatier-Senderens)

$$ \mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Pt/Pd/Ni}} \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_3} $$ $$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Pt/Pd/Ni}} \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_2{-}CH_3} $$
🔬 [Mechanism & HOTS] Hydrogenation

Mechanism: H2 adsorbs onto metal catalyst surface → H–H bond weakens → syn addition.

HOTS: ① Why Pt/Pd preferred over Ni? Room temp vs ~200°C. ② Why syn addition? Both H's from same catalyst face. ③ Lindlar's catalyst gives cis-alkenes.

⚡ PYQ: Sabatier-Senderens, Lindlar's catalyst

2. From Alkyl Halides (Reduction)

$$ \mathrm{CH_3Cl} + \mathrm{H_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Zn/HCl}} \mathrm{CH_4} + \mathrm{HCl} $$

3. Wurtz Reaction

$$ 2\mathrm{CH_3Br} + 2\mathrm{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_3} + 2\mathrm{NaBr} $$
🔬 [Mechanism & HOTS] Wurtz Reaction

Mechanism (Free Radical): R–X + Na → R• + NaX; R• + Na → R⁻Na⁺; R⁻Na⁺ + R–X → R–R + NaX

HOTS: ① Why only even-numbered alkanes? Two identical alkyl fragments couple. ② Two different RX? Mixture of 3 products. ③ Why dry ether? Water reacts with Na violently.

⚡ CBSE PYQ: Why can't Wurtz prepare methane? Requires HX → gives H2 gas.

⚡ PYQ: Wurtz limitations

4. Decarboxylation (Soda Lime)

$$ \mathrm{CH_3COONa} + \mathrm{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{CaO},\;\Delta} \mathrm{CH_4} + \mathrm{Na_2CO_3} $$

5. Kolbe's Electrolytic Method

$$ 2\mathrm{CH_3COO^-} \xrightarrow[-2\mathrm{e}^-]{\text{Anode}} \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_3} + 2\mathrm{CO_2} $$
🔬 [HOTS] Kolbe's Electrolysis

Mechanism: Anode: CH3COO⁻ → CH3COO• + e⁻; 2 CH3COO• → CH3–CH3 + 2CO2. Cathode: 2H2O + 2e⁻ → H2 + 2OH⁻

HOTS: ① Why can't methane be prepared by Kolbe's? HCOONa → H• → H–H. ② Kolbe's gives even C alkanes (like Wurtz).

6. From Grignard Reagent

$$ \mathrm{RMgX} + \mathrm{H_2O} \xrightarrow{} \mathrm{R-H} + \mathrm{Mg(OH)X} $$

⚗️ Physical Properties

  • State: C1–C4 (gases), C5–C17 (liquids), >C17 (solids)
  • Solubility: Insoluble in water (non-polar), soluble in organic solvents
  • Boiling point: Increases with molecular mass; branched isomers have lower bp
  • Melting point: Increases irregularly; odd-even alternation effect

🔥 Chemical Properties

1. Halogenation (Free Radical Substitution)

$$ \mathrm{CH_4} + \mathrm{Cl_2} \xrightarrow{h\nu\;\text{or}\;\Delta} \mathrm{CH_3Cl} + \mathrm{HCl} $$
🔬 [Mechanism] Free Radical Halogenation

Mechanism:

① Initiation: Cl2 → 2 Cl• (homolytic cleavage)

② Propagation: Cl• + CH4 → •CH3 + HCl; •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•

③ Termination: Cl• + Cl• → Cl2; •CH3 + •CH3 → CH3–CH3; •CH3 + Cl• → CH3Cl

HOTS: ① Chlorination is not very selective (Cl• highly reactive). ② Bromination selective for 3° > 2° > 1° (Br• less reactive). ③ Reactivity order: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2.

⚡ PYQ: Bromination selectivity

2. Combustion

$$ \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2} + \left(\frac{3n+1}{2}\right)\mathrm{O_2} \rightarrow n\mathrm{CO_2} + (n+1)\mathrm{H_2O} + \text{energy} $$

3. Pyrolysis (Cracking)

$$ \mathrm{C}_n\mathrm{H}_{2n+2} \xrightarrow{500-700^\circ\mathrm{C}} \text{Lower alkanes} + \text{Alkenes} + \mathrm{H_2} $$

4. Aromatization (Catalytic Reforming)

$$ \mathrm{C_6H_{14}} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cr_2O_3/Al_2O_3},\;500^\circ\mathrm{C}} \mathrm{C_6H_6} + 4\mathrm{H_2} $$ ⚡ PYQ: Aromatization

🔬 Conformations of Ethane

Conformations are different spatial arrangements achieved by rotation about C–C single bonds. The rotational barrier in ethane is ~12.5 kJ mol⁻¹.

Newman & Sawhorse Projections

  • Eclipsed (θ = 0°, 360°): H atoms overlap → maximum torsional strain (~12.5 kJ mol⁻¹ above staggered). Highest energy.
  • Staggered (θ = 60°): H atoms offset → minimum torsional strain. Lowest energy, most stable.
  • Skew (θ = 120°): Intermediate energy between eclipsed and staggered.

At room temperature, molecules rotate ~10¹⁰ times/sec. Population of staggered conformer ~99% at 25°C.

🔬 [HOTS] Conformational Analysis

Why is eclipsed higher in energy? Torsional strain — repulsion between C–H bonding electron clouds on adjacent carbons. In staggered form, C–H bonds are maximally separated.

🧪 Alkenes (CnH2n)

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing C=C double bonds (sp2 hybridized). They undergo electrophilic addition reactions.

Geometrical Isomerism (cis / trans)

Due to restricted rotation about C=C, cis-trans isomerism occurs when each doubly-bonded carbon has two different substituents.

$$ \text{cis-but-2-ene: } \underset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{\overset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{\mathrm{C}}}\!=\!\underset{\mathrm{H}}{\overset{\mathrm{H}}{\mathrm{C}}} \qquad \text{trans-but-2-ene: } \underset{\mathrm{H}}{\overset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{\mathrm{C}}}\!=\!\underset{\mathrm{CH_3}}{\overset{\mathrm{H}}{\mathrm{C}}} $$

trans-isomer is more stable (less steric hindrance).

⚡ PYQ: Conditions for geometrical isomerism

Preparation of Alkenes

a) Dehydrohalogenation

$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_2Br} + \mathrm{KOH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{alc.},\;\Delta} \mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{KBr} + \mathrm{H_2O} $$

Saytzeff's rule: More substituted alkene is major product.

b) Dehydration of Alcohols

$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_2OH} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H_2SO_4},\;443\;\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} $$

c) Dehalogenation of Vicinal Dihalides

$$ \mathrm{CH_2Br{-}CH_2Br} + \mathrm{Zn} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{ZnBr_2} $$

d) Partial Hydrogenation of Alkynes

$$ \mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} + \mathrm{H_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Lindlar's}} cis\text{-}\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} $$ $$ \mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} + 2\mathrm{H} \xrightarrow{\text{Na/liq.NH_3}} trans\text{-}\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} $$ ⚡ PYQ: Lindlar's vs Na/NH3

⚡ Chemical Properties of Alkenes

1. Electrophilic Addition (Markovnikov's Rule)

Rule: Positive part (H) adds to carbon with more H atoms; negative part (X) to carbon with fewer H atoms.

$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{HBr} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH_3{-}CHBr{-}CH_3} \quad (\text{2-bromopropane}) $$

Step 1: H⁺ attacks CH₂ (more H atoms) → 2° carbocation (more stable)

$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{H^+} \rightarrow [\mathrm{CH_3{-}}\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H{-}CH_3}] $$

Step 2: Br⁻ attacks carbocation → 2-bromopropane

$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}}\overset{+}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H{-}CH_3} + \mathrm{Br^-} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH_3{-}CHBr{-}CH_3} $$

2. Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide Effect / Kharasch)

$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{HBr} \xrightarrow{(\mathrm{PhCO_2})_2} \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH_2{-}CH_2Br} $$
🔬 [Mechanism & HOTS] Peroxide Effect

Mechanism (Free Radical):

Initiation: RO–OR → 2 RO•; RO• + H–Br → ROH + Br•

Propagation: Br• + CH3–CH=CH2 → CH3–ĊH–CH2Br (2° radical, more stable); CH3–ĊH–CH2Br + H–Br → CH3–CH2–CH2Br + Br•

HOTS: ① Why only HBr? HCl bond too strong (431 kJ/mol), HI bond too weak (297 kJ/mol). HBr (366 kJ/mol) is optimal. ② Br• adds to less substituted carbon (CH2) to form more stable 2° radical.

⚡ CBSE PYQ: Why doesn't HI show peroxide effect despite weak bond?

⚡ PYQ: Peroxide effect, why only HBr

3. Addition of Halogens

$$ \mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{Br_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{CCl_4}} \mathrm{CH_2Br{-}CH_2Br} $$

Reddish-orange Br2 decolorized — test for unsaturation.

4. Ozonolysis

$$ \mathrm{R_2C{=}CR'_2} + \mathrm{O_3} \rightarrow \text{Ozonide} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Zn/H_2O}} 2\mathrm{R_2C{=}O} $$ ⚡ PYQ: Product identification from ozonolysis

5. Baeyer's Test (KMnO4)

$$ 3\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2} + 2\mathrm{KMnO_4} + 4\mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow 3\mathrm{CH_2OH{-}CH_2OH} + 2\mathrm{MnO_2} + 2\mathrm{KOH} $$

🧪 Alkynes (CnH2n-2)

Acidity of Terminal Alkynes

H attached to triply bonded C is acidic (pKa ~25). sp C has 50% s-character → more polarized C–H bond → stable conjugate base.

$$ \mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} + \mathrm{AgNO_3} + \mathrm{NH_4OH} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH{\equiv}C^-Ag^+}{\downarrow}\;(\text{white}) $$ $$ \mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} + \mathrm{Cu_2Cl_2} + \mathrm{NH_4OH} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH{\equiv}C^-Cu^+}{\downarrow}\;(\text{red}) $$ ⚡ PYQ: Acidity trend, silver/copper acetylide test

Preparation

$$ \mathrm{CaC_2} + 2\mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} + \mathrm{Ca(OH)_2} $$

Cyclic Polymerization

$$ 3\mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} \xrightarrow{\text{red hot Fe tube, }873\;\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{C_6H_6} $$

🧪 Aromatic Hydrocarbons (Arenes)

Structure of Benzene

Planar, cyclic C6H6. All C–C bonds identical (139 pm). Resonance hybrid of two Kekulé structures. Delocalization of 6 π-electrons → resonance energy ~150 kJ mol⁻¹.

Aromaticity (Hückel's Rule)

A planar, cyclic, conjugated molecule with (4n+2) π-electrons is aromatic.

  • Benzene: 6π (n=1) → Aromatic
  • Naphthalene: 10π (n=2) → Aromatic
  • Cyclooctatetraene: 8π → Not aromatic (non-planar, anti-aromatic)
⚡ PYQ: Hückel's rule (4n+2)

Electrophilic Substitution Reactions (EAS)

General mechanism: ① Generate E⁺ ② E⁺ attacks benzene → σ-complex (arenium ion) ③ Deprotonation → aromaticity restored.

a) Nitration

$$ \mathrm{C_6H_6} + \mathrm{HNO_3} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H_2SO_4},\;323-333\;\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{C_6H_5NO_2} + \mathrm{H_2O} $$

E⁺: NO2+ (nitronium ion, from HNO3 + 2H2SO4)

b) Halogenation

$$ \mathrm{C_6H_6} + \mathrm{Cl_2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{FeCl_3},\;\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5Cl} + \mathrm{HCl} $$

c) Sulphonation

$$ \mathrm{C_6H_6} + \mathrm{H_2SO_4} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5SO_3H} + \mathrm{H_2O} $$

d) Friedel-Crafts Alkylation

$$ \mathrm{C_6H_6} + \mathrm{CH_3Cl} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{AlCl_3},\;\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5CH_3} + \mathrm{HCl} $$

e) Friedel-Crafts Acylation

$$ \mathrm{C_6H_6} + \mathrm{CH_3COCl} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{AlCl_3},\;\Delta} \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3} + \mathrm{HCl} $$
🔬 [Mechanism & HOTS] EAS

Why substitution not addition? Addition would destroy aromaticity (~150 kJ/mol loss). Substitution preserves the aromatic ring.

σ-complex (arenium ion): 4 π-electrons delocalized over ring (NOT aromatic). Positive charge at ortho/para positions.

Directive influence:

① Activating, o/p-directing: –NH2, –OH, –OCH3, –CH3 (electron-donating)

② Deactivating, m-directing: –NO2, –CN, –CHO, –COOH (electron-withdrawing)

③ Halogens: o/p-directing but deactivating (unique case)

⚡ CBSE PYQ: Why is –NH2 activating but –NO2 deactivating?

⚡ PYQ: EAS mechanism, directive effects

Carcinogenicity of Polynuclear Hydrocarbons

PAHs like benzopyrene (cigarette smoke, coal tar) are potent carcinogens. Formed during incomplete combustion.

📚 NCERT Solutions Vault

All chapter-end exercises from NCERT Class 11 Chemistry — Hydrocarbons, with detailed solutions.

1What are the products of ozonolysis of propene?
$$ \mathrm{CH_3{-}CH{=}CH_2} + \mathrm{O_3} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Zn/H_2O}} \mathrm{CH_3CHO} + \mathrm{HCHO} $$

Products: Acetaldehyde (CH3CHO) and Formaldehyde (HCHO).

2How can you distinguish between ethene and ethyne?

Silver mirror test: Ethyne gives white precipitate of Ag-acetylide with ammoniacal AgNO3. Ethene does not react.

$$ \mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} + 2\mathrm{AgNO_3} + 2\mathrm{NH_4OH} \rightarrow \mathrm{AgC{\equiv}CAg}{\downarrow} + 2\mathrm{NH_4NO_3} + 2\mathrm{H_2O} $$
3Arrange in decreasing order of boiling points: n-butane, 2-methylbutane, n-pentane, 2,2-dimethylpropane.

Order: n-pentane (309 K) > 2-methylbutane (301 K) > 2,2-dimethylpropane (282.5 K) > n-butane (272.5 K)

Reason: Boiling point depends on molecular mass and branching. More branching → more compact → lower surface area → weaker van der Waals forces.

4Arrange F2, Cl2, Br2, I2 in order of increasing reactivity with alkanes.

Increasing reactivity: I2 < Br2 < Cl2 < F2

Reason: Reactivity depends on bond dissociation energy. F–F is weakest (158 kJ/mol) → most reactive. I–I bond is weakest but reaction is reversible and negligible.

5Increasing order of reduction of alkyl halides with Zn/dil HCl?

Bond dissociation energy: C–Cl (351) > C–Br (293) > C–I (234) kJ/mol

Order: R–Cl < R–Br < R–I. Option (ii) is correct.

6Correct IUPAC name: 3,6-Diethyl-2-methyloctane / 5-Isopropyl-3-ethyloctane / 3-Ethyl-5-isopropyloctane / 3-Isopropyl-6-ethyloctane?

Draw each structure. The longest chain has 8 carbons. The correct name is 3-Ethyl-5-isopropyloctane.

7Addition of HBr to 1-butene gives mixture of A (1-bromobutane), B (2-bromobutane), C (1-bromo-2-methylpropane). Which is major?

B (2-bromobutane) is major — Markovnikov product via 2° carbocation.

C involves hydride shift (rearrangement) — minor.

A is anti-Markovnikov product — minor without peroxide.

8Which compound will NOT show geometrical isomerism?

Geometrical isomerism requires two different substituents on each doubly bonded carbon. Compounds where one C has two identical groups (e.g., CH2=CHCl) show no geometrical isomerism.

9Arrange HCl, HBr, HI in decreasing reactivity with propene.

Bond strength: H–Cl (431) > H–Br (366) > H–I (297) kJ/mol

Weaker bond → more reactive. Order: HI > HBr > HCl

10Arrange carbanions in decreasing stability: (A) H3C–C≡C⁻ (B) H–C≡C⁻ (C) CH3–CH2⁻

Stability depends on s-character of carbon bearing charge. sp (50%) > sp² (33%) > sp³ (25%).

B > A > C (Option ii). Both A and B are sp hybridized, but CH3 group in A destabilizes slightly by hyperconjugation.

11Decreasing order of rate of β-elimination with alc. KOH?

The rate depends on stability of the alkene formed (Saytzeff). More substituted alkene → faster elimination.

3° > 2° > 1° alkyl halides for elimination.

12Which reaction of methane is incomplete combustion?

Option (iii) CH4 + O2 → C + 2H2O is incomplete combustion (soot formation).

Option (iv) is complete combustion. Options (i) and (ii) are controlled oxidation.

13Which methane oxidations are controlled oxidations?

Controlled oxidation uses specific catalysts for partial oxidation:

(iii) CH4 + O2 → HCHO + H2O (MoO3 catalyst) — controlled oxidation

(i) Complete combustion; (ii) Incomplete combustion — not controlled.

14Which conformer of ethane is most stable? Explain.

The staggered conformer (θ = 60°) is most stable. C–H bonds are 60° apart → minimum torsional strain.

The eclipsed conformer (θ = 0°) is ~12.5 kJ mol⁻¹ higher in energy due to C–H bond electron cloud repulsion.

15Write Newman projections for eclipsed and staggered ethane.

Staggered: Front H at 60° offset from back H. Eclipsed: Front H exactly overlap back H.

In Newman projection: front C = dot (•), back C = circle (○). Bonds from front C drawn to edge of circle; bonds from back C drawn to circle circumference.

16Which has highest bp: n-butane, isobutane, n-pentane?

n-Pentane (bp 309 K) — highest molecular mass (5 C atoms).

n-Butane (272.5 K), isobutane (261 K) — only 4 C atoms.

17Products of ozonolysis of 1-butene and 2-butene?

1-butene: CH3CH2CH=CH2 → CH3CH2CHO (propanal) + HCHO (formaldehyde)

2-butene: CH3CH=CHCH3 → 2 CH3CHO (acetaldehyde)

18Why is benzene extraordinarily stable despite high unsaturation?

① Resonance stabilization (~150 kJ mol⁻¹) — 6 π-electrons delocalized over ring.

② Hückel's rule (4n+2, n=1) — aromatic.

③ Addition would destroy aromaticity; substitution preserves it.

19Convert ethanoic acid into benzene.

CH3COOH → Decarboxylation → CH4 → Chlorination → CH3Cl → Wurtz → C2H6 → Dehydrogenation → C2H4 → C2H2 → Cyclic polymerization (873 K, Fe tube) → C6H6

$$ 3\mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} \xrightarrow{\text{red hot Fe, }873\;\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{C_6H_6} $$
20Write mechanism of free radical chlorination of methane.

Initiation: Cl2 → 2 Cl•

Propagation: Cl• + CH4 → •CH3 + HCl; •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•

Termination: Cl• + Cl• → Cl2; •CH3 + •CH3 → CH3–CH3; •CH3 + Cl• → CH3Cl

21What happens when benzene is treated with CH3Cl in presence of AlCl3? Write mechanism.

Friedel-Crafts alkylation: C6H6 + CH3Cl → C6H5CH3 (toluene) + HCl

Step 1: CH3Cl + AlCl3 → CH3⁺ + AlCl4⁻

Step 2: C6H6 + CH3⁺ → [C6H6–CH3]⁺ (arenium ion)

Step 3: [C6H6–CH3]⁺ + AlCl4⁻ → C6H5CH3 + AlCl3 + HCl

22Distinguish between n-propyl bromide and isopropyl bromide.

Both give propene on dehydrohalogenation (same product). Alternative: Wurtz reaction — n-propyl bromide gives n-hexane; isopropyl bromide gives 2,3-dimethylbutane.

Or: React with Na/dry ether and compare products by GC or boiling point.

23What happens when but-2-ene is treated with alkaline KMnO4?

Cold, dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent) oxidizes but-2-ene to vicinal diol (butane-2,3-diol).

Purple color fades, brown MnO2 precipitates — test for unsaturation.

Hot concentrated KMnO4 → oxidative cleavage to carboxylic acids.

24Products of Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with CH3COCl?

Acetophenone (methyl phenyl ketone, C6H5COCH3)

CH3COCl + AlCl3 → CH3CO⁺ (acylium ion, resonance stabilized) + AlCl4⁻

25Effect of branching on melting point of alkanes?

Branched alkanes have higher melting points than straight-chain isomers (opposite to boiling point).

Reason: More symmetrical, compact molecules pack better in crystal lattice → stronger intermolecular forces.

Example: n-pentane (mp 143 K) vs 2,2-dimethylpropane (mp 257 K).

26Why does propanol have higher boiling point than butane?

Propanol (CH3CH2CH2OH, bp 370 K) has –OH group capable of intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

Butane (C4H10, bp 272.5 K) is non-polar — only weak van der Waals forces.

This shows functional groups can dominate boiling point trends over molecular size.

27Convert ethyne to benzene.

Pass ethyne through red hot iron tube at 873 K. 3 molecules trimerize to form benzene.

$$ 3\mathrm{CH{\equiv}CH} \xrightarrow{\text{red hot Fe, }873\;\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{C_6H_6} $$
28What is the peroxide effect? Explain with example.

Peroxide effect (Kharasch): In presence of organic peroxides, HBr adds to unsymmetrical alkenes in anti-Markovnikov manner.

Propene + HBr + benzoyl peroxide → 1-bromopropane

Mechanism: free radical chain (Br• adds to CH2 → 2° radical intermediate).

Only with HBr — HCl bond too strong, HI bond too weak.

29What is soda lime? Its role in decarboxylation?

Soda lime: Mixture of NaOH and CaO.

Role: When sodium salt of carboxylic acid is heated with soda lime, CO2 is eliminated → alkane with one carbon less.

CaO makes mixture porous, prevents fusion, absorbs moisture, improves heat distribution.

30Explain mechanism of nitration of benzene.

C6H6 + HNO3 (conc. H2SO4, 323-333 K) → C6H5NO2 + H2O

Step 1: HNO3 + H2SO4 → NO2⁺ + H3O⁺ + 2HSO4⁻

Step 2: C6H6 + NO2⁺ → [C6H6–NO2]⁺ (σ-complex, arenium ion)

Step 3: [C6H6–NO2]⁺ + HSO4⁻ → C6H5NO2 + H2SO4

31Why can't methane be prepared by Wurtz reaction?

Wurtz: 2RX + 2Na → R–R + 2NaX (forms alkane with even number of C atoms).

Methane requires R = H (not an alkyl group). HX + Na → H2 gas, not CH4.

Smallest alkane by Wurtz = ethane (C2H6).

32Arrange in order of decreasing acidity: ethane, ethene, ethyne.

Ethyne > Ethene > Ethane

Acidity ∝ s-character: sp (50%) > sp² (33%) > sp³ (25%)

pKa values: ethyne (~25), ethene (~44), ethane (~50)

33An alkene on ozonolysis gives CH3CHO and HCHO. Identify.

Working backwards: CH3CHO corresponds to CH3–CH= ; HCHO corresponds to =CH2

Alkene: Propene (CH3–CH=CH2)

34An alkene gives CH3CH2CHO and CH3CHO on ozonolysis. Identify.

CH3CH2CHO corresponds to CH3CH2–CH= ; CH3CHO corresponds to CH3–CH=

Alkene: Pent-2-ene (CH3CH2–CH=CH–CH3)

35What are arenes? Give examples.

Arenes (aromatic hydrocarbons) contain one or more benzene rings. Named from Greek aroma (pleasant odor).

Examples: Benzene (C6H6), Toluene (C6H5CH3), Naphthalene (C10H8).

36IUPAC names: (i) CH3–CH=CH2 (ii) CH3–C≡C–CH3

(i) Propene (3 carbons, double bond at C-1)

(ii) But-2-yne (4 carbons, triple bond at C-2)

37What is Markovnikov's rule? Explain with example.

Rule: When an unsymmetrical reagent (HX) adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, H attaches to the C with more H atoms, X to C with fewer H atoms.

Example: Propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane (via 2° carbocation, more stable).

Reason: Reaction proceeds through the more stable carbocation intermediate.

38Which is more stable: cis-2-butene or trans-2-butene? Why?

trans-2-Butene is more stable. In cis isomer, two CH3 groups are on same side → steric hindrance. In trans, CH3 groups are opposite → minimized steric repulsion.

Energy difference: ~4.8 kJ mol⁻¹.
