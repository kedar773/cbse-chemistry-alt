Trend Definition & Pattern Across Period (L→R) Down Group (T→B) Reason Magnitude Range General Order PYQ Tags JEE Level
First Ionization Energy (IE₁) Energy required to remove 1 e⁻ from neutral atom (M → M⁺ + e⁻); measure of "pull" on valence electron Increases (H: 1312 kJ/mol → He: 2372 kJ/mol; exceptions at Be→B & Mg→Al due to orbital filling) Decreases (alkali metals: Li: 520 → Na: 496 → K: 419 → Rb: 403 → Cs: 375 kJ/mol) Across: increasing protons, same shell → stronger nuclear pull. Down: more shells, shielding → weaker pull Noble gases >> all others (He: 2372, Ne: 2080, Ar: 1521, Kr: 1351, Xe: 1170 kJ/mol; all very high) He > Ne > F > N > Li; Order: noble gases (highest) >> halogens >> alkaline earths >> alkali metals (lowest) 2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 ⭐⭐⭐
Second Ionization Energy (IE₂) Energy to remove 2nd e⁻ from +1 ion (M⁺ → M²⁺ + e⁻); much higher than IE₁ (removing e⁻ from filled shell is harder) Increases across period (but discontinuities at group boundaries; huge jump when moving from valence to inner shell) Generally increases as going down (similar trend to IE₁ but offset) Removing second e⁻ breaks into inner shell (lower n) → much higher energy required For alkali metals: IE₂ >> IE₁ (e.g., Na: IE₁ = 496, IE₂ = 4563 kJ/mol; ~9× higher) Alkaline earths have moderate IE₂ (break into filled p-shell); alkali metals have huge IE₂ (break into filled noble gas configuration) 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022 ⭐⭐
Successive Ionization Energy Jumps Large jump when removing e⁻ from stable shell; indicates shell boundaries Mg (IE₁ ≈ 738, IE₂ ≈ 1451, IE₃ ≈ 7733 kJ/mol): huge jump at IE₃ (removing from filled 2p into 2s) Varying pattern for different elements; Group 2 shows regular pattern (large jump at IE₃) When electron removed from filled/half-filled orbital, pulling the next e⁻ from more stable orbital is much harder Jump factor: IE₃/IE₂ ~ 5-10 for alkali metals (moving from valence to next inner shell) — 2024, 2023, 2022 ⭐⭐
Electron Affinity (EA) Energy change when e⁻ added to neutral atom (M + e⁻ → M⁻); measure of "attraction" for incoming e⁻ Generally increases (more protons pull e⁻ in); exceptions where half-filled orbitals are stable (B, Mg, N, P do not readily gain e⁻) Generally decreases (larger atoms, more shielding, weaker attraction to incoming e⁻) Across: more protons, same shell. Down: more shells, more shielding. Half-filled p/d orbitals: very weak EA (stable; don't want extra e⁻) Halogens: most negative (F: −328, Cl: −349, Br: −325, I: −295 kJ/mol; strong attraction for 1 e⁻); alkali metals: slightly negative (like e⁻; don't want more) Halogens (most negative EA) >> transition metals >> alkali metals (least negative/slightly positive); varies by element 2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 ⭐⭐⭐
Electronegativity (EN) Ability of atom to attract shared electrons in covalent bond; Pauling scale (0-4) Increases across period Decreases down group Across: same reason as EA/IE₁. Down: valence electrons farther from nucleus; weaker pull F: 3.98 (most electronegative), H: 2.20, C: 2.55, N: 3.04, O: 3.44, Cl: 3.16, Br: 2.96, I: 2.66, Li: 0.98 (least electronegative among metals) F > O > N > Cl > Br > I (trends across & down); halogens >> alkali metals; noble gases not assigned (rarely form bonds) 2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 ⭐⭐⭐
Metallic Character Tendency to lose electrons; form cations; conduct electricity; luster; malleability Decreases across period (more electronegative, harder to lose e⁻) Increases down group (larger atoms, more shielding, easier to lose valence e⁻) Across: increasing proton pull on valence e⁻. Down: increasing shell #, easier to lose outer e⁻ Alkali metals (Group 1) most metallic; metalloids at border (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te); nonmetals (Group 13-18 right side) not metallic Metallic character: Group 1 >> Group 2 >> Groups 3-13 >> metalloids (14-17 right side) >> nonmetals; decreases right/up on table 2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 ⭐⭐⭐
Nonmetallic Character (Opposite of Metallic) Tendency to gain electrons; form anions; poor conductors (except nonmetal semi-conductors); dull Increases across period Decreases down group (easier to lose e⁻ as you go down, so less tendency to gain e⁻) Opposite of metallic character; halogens gain 1 e⁻ easily (need 1 to complete octet) Halogens most nonmetallic; noble gases inert (neither metallic nor clearly nonmetallic); metalloids intermediate Nonmetallic character: Halogens (Group 17) >> Chalcogens (Group 16) >> Group 15 >> metalloids >> metals; increases left/down diagonal from F corner 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022 ⭐⭐

Answer: O²⁻ (largest, ~140 pm) > F⁻ (~133 pm) > Na⁺ (~102 pm) > Ne (~62 pm, very small as neutral atom). The isoelectronic trend: same # electrons; size ∝ 1/Z (approximately).
